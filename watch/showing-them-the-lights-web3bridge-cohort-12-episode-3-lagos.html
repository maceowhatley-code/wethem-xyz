A front-end developer who wanted to build, not consume.

Godswill, Lagos, 8 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

Eight minutes in Ikorodu, Lagos, recorded at the end of Web3Bridge Cohort 12, the third episode in a run of short interviews We Them Media filmed with the developers finishing the course. The room is borrowed: the interviewer thanks the subject and his roommates for letting the camera occupy it for the night.

Web3Bridge founder Ayodeji Awosika opens from the same building, counting who came into the course and who finished, and reporting applications for the next cohort “from Cameroon, from Togo, from Kenya, from Zimbabwe, from Uganda”.

Then the introduction. “I’m Godswill Udolo”, he says, “popularly known as G-Will”, Igbo by tribe, a front-end developer who got in through a friend, a senior dev.

What he arrived with was a narrow picture of the field. “before now, I was wondering what’s a smart contract, how to interact with blockchain”, he says, and “I usually thought blockchain was just basically Bitcoin, Polygon, Solana”. The course widened it: “I was taught how to like write this smart contract, make my own tokens, interact with blockchain”.

He had braced for something cheaper, mentors who “just give you, um, PDF, just give you normal videos to just go and watch”. Instead, “we had practical experience. We had live mentors.” Asked whether it was an easy choice to commit to coming, he gives the shortest answer on the tape: “There was no easy choice for me.”

On why the technology matters where he lives, he keeps it domestic. “Smart contract is just basically like your lawyer, you don’t need this lawyer”, written code that executes when it is meant to, and with it the fact that “there’s transparency in a blockchain”. The image he lands on is his own: “So you introducing them to the blockchain is like showing them the lights. So everybody wants to see the lights.”

Ambition is plainly put. “I hope to make my impact in the world. I hope to onboard more users to Web3.” And on which side of crypto he wants to stand: “I want to be the builder”, for “that feeling where you make something and people use it, you have this feeling of satisfaction, not just consuming what has been there”.

His team’s project is Race3, which he describes as “just you connecting founders and investors together”, with funding released against milestones rather than in one lump.

The last stretch is about where developers talk. He joined the channel’s Discord and then Warpcast as soon as each was mentioned, because “there’s a lot of noise on X” while the smaller room is all devs, people who relate to what you post. He collects communities, he says, and this one made sense.
