A first interview, and advice back to Newark.

Fournier · Reported by Maceo · Denver · 10 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

Ten minutes at ETH Denver 2024, one of the interviews We Them Media filmed in Colorado that week. The subject is Fournier, who came out from Newark, stayed in the house with the crew, and says plainly near the top that “this is the first interview I think I’ve done ever”. The host explains on tape how they met: a Dot Connector event in New York back in September, then a conversation about coming to Denver, then a spot in the B&B.

He starts with what the week gave him. “I learned more about crypto than I anticipated that I learned”, he says, and the surprise was less the technology than the company he was already keeping. “I realized I was part of communities that, you know, it was more going on than what meets the eye.” He is in the second cohort of the Near Protocol Horizon Accelerator, and what Denver did for that was turn a screen into a room. “It’s been great just meeting everybody in real life so far”, he says, because “you usually work digitally”, a lot of writing and note-taking and testing things out, and because everybody on his team is “remote all over the world in different places”. Meeting them, he says, “puts the cherry on top”.

His takeaways are about skills rather than tokens. “there’s always more to learn about what you can do”, and the second half of that is position: “realize the skills you have and realize when you lead and when you follow”. He also thinks most of the applications are still ahead, that “there’s really a lot of different ways that crypto is being applied now, yet, but it still hasn’t been applied”.

The origin story is short. He invested “earlier than that in the bull run in like 2017”, caught Bitcoin at what he calls a decent price, watched it run to twenty thousand dollars, got out, then joined PubDAO and started treating crypto “seriously, like, job-wise, in probably about 2021”. Asked for a core memory, he does not pick a trade. He picks watching the people he knows grow.

The last question is what he would tell somebody back in Newark who knows nothing about any of it. “the internet is the most powerful thing, but it’s also the most dangerous thing”, so “research, teach yourself as much as you can”, and “recognize what gifts you have and use those to your advantage”. Then the part about other people: “Make sure that it’s a win-win situation with everybody you’re working with.” He finishes on the block itself. You can stay on it for twenty-five years, or you can get a passport, and he puts both down as choices rather than verdicts.
