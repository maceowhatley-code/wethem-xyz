An album, a five person team and a sick day.

Life of Thom, New York, 15 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

Fifteen minutes in New York with an independent artist a few days after his album release party, taped at the Station 3 space courtesy of Archive. Life of Thom is four days into being ill and turned up anyway, which he says out loud in the first minute: “I feel like shit low-key because I’ve been sick for like the last four days, but I’m Gucci.”

The route in was open mics. “Tapped in with a bunch of open mics”, he says, and from there a scene of people putting on their own events. The step change came from leaving town: “the crossover was really like when I went to South by Southwest for the first time”. After that it was volume and consistency, “playing all around New York, you know, selling out most of these venues for real”.

What the new record means to him is not applause, it is order. Success, he says, is everything running the way he set it up: “all my shit’s on check, you know, team’s on check, this rollout’s on check”. The interview keeps returning to that. He runs his work out of Notion, he thinks about the business like an operator, and he has built a team of about five or six around it. “it’s about a good five six people um I got them split up in all different roles”, with day to day people, overseers pitching labels and brand partners on the West Coast, and a lawyer.

His view of what an artist owes that team is the sharpest thing on the tape. He is not outsourcing the load, he is absorbing it so everyone else can move faster: “I want to make everyone’s positions As easy as possible so they can do Way more for me”. And on where it starts: “the groundwork has to come from the artist always”.

The Web3 part of his story is treated as one more thing to learn rather than a conversion. “Web3 was a really nice curve in the learning”, he says, and describes seeing it as a place to build financially and to be early: “I want to be like one of the head honchos”. He is clear about why that matters to him beyond money, which is to “kind of like give that inspiration to others in the creative space”, and about how it felt at the time: “it’s a Pandora’s box that really got unlocked”. He credits two people by name for the door, his guy Matt Bond, and LaTosha, who he dropped a record with back in 2018. “shouts LaTosha man that’s that’s really my big sister”.

He is also honest that the fit is unsettled, and asks aloud whether the protocol layer really matches the way the music industry works. His answer is to keep it in hand either way and keep building outside it.

The last stretch is what is coming. A single with an artist from Harlem, merch, production sent out for placements, something he cannot announce, and “There is a deluxe for the project we just put out”. Asked to close on the album title, he explains Room 44 through numerology, four standing for order, two fours making eight, eight turned on its side making infinity. “It felt like I was stepping into a timeless zone”, he says, “It’s an infinite sparring session, low-key”. His favourite song on it is Shabbat with CJ Fly, which he notes has left a lot of people with the wrong impression about his religion.

What he says he is most excited about is not a release at all. “In general, it’s just the experience And what new characters is we about to meet”.
