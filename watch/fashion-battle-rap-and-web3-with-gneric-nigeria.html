Sewing, battle rap and a grant that actually landed.

Gneric, Reported by Maceo, Nigeria, 38 min

To quote this piece: Gneric, “Sewing, battle rap and a grant that actually landed,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/fashion-battle-rap-and-web3-with-gneric-nigeria. Each transcript line links to its second.

Thirty eight minutes in Nigeria with a musician who is also a fashion designer, a poet and a battle rapper. It was recorded on the trip We Them Media made to Lagos and Abuja to sit with the Creative Alchemy community, where Gneric is a member. The conversation runs from the shop where she learned to sew to the EP and the clothing brand she is finishing this year.

She came in through a friend who would not stop talking about the community, and through the word Web3, which she had found a few weeks earlier and could not get anyone around her to hear. “It felt like I was speaking an alien language that nobody understood.”

Fashion came first through a job she had to quit. “I had to stop my job that I was doing because I realized that it drew me away from fashion.” She was working at a store called Fashion Town, selling accessories and fabrics, and that is where the sewing started. Then the owners opened a second shop selling drinks and food and asked her to manage both. She describes what that did to her plainly: “I felt like there’s something I’m supposed to be doing and I’m not doing it.” The pull has always been the same one. “I’ve always loved the idea of piecing several pieces of outfit together, putting them on and looking really good.”

Music is older than the fashion. She joined the choir as a child, and made music for years with no idea there was money in it. “I didn’t even know I could make money for my music.” She puts the discovery at two years before this was filmed, and she is careful about what changed and what did not: “It wasn’t really about the money.”

Rap is where the writing came from. She names Eminem and the Nigerian rapper Eva Alodia, and says what drew her to hip hop was how much a rapper can fit in. “I loved how rappers were able to say a lot more than people that just sing songs.” Battle rap arrived as text, on the app To Go, then Facebook, then Naira Land, then a WhatsApp group, and only after that as video: “we’re doing video battles so you record your rounds”. There were criteria, she says: points for openers, closers, punchlines, word plays, metaphors.

The song she is writing at the time of filming is made of questions. “I’m questioning a lot of things I’m questioning what life is I’m questioning where I am right now.” Asked how it feels inside all that, she says she feels lost.

What she likes about the space is not the technology. “The togetherness and the unity that I’ve seen in Web3 Is mind-blowing.” It has also been practical. A Twitter thread led to a space, which led to a group chat, and somebody in it told her to “look out for grants”. She applied to Mint Fund, which she explains as “Mint Fund, they fund creatives that are coming into Web3 to mint their first NFTs”, and it worked: “I applied for it and then they called me and I got it.”

On the community she is blunt about who it belongs to: “The community is not about one person. It’s about every single person within the community.” Her own year has two items on it, an EP and a brand: “I’m going to be officially launching Vibes on Styles this year.”

She closes with the strongest thing on the tape, talking to anyone making anything. “Just keep creating, don’t stop creating Because that’s the truest form of freedom”. Then the part that has teeth: “If you stop, you’re not stopping for anyone There’s nobody behind you, there’s nobody in front of you”. People will try to help, she says, but “you’re on that lonely road And you have to keep travelling”.
