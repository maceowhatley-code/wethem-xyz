Two locals on Red Rocks, bass and the shows every night.

Duncan and Hannah, Denver, 11 min, filmed 3 March 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Eleven minutes on a Denver street at night, with two people who had just come out of a friend’s show. The camera belongs to somebody else, the interviewer is in town for a crypto conference, and the first minute goes on getting it pointed at faces.

Duncan and Hannah give their names and the split is immediate. He was born and raised here. She says “I wasn’t born here, but I moved here when I was five.” Asked how they see the city, he answers for both of them: “It’s growing fast, but it’s super fun. I mean, we’re here mostly for the music scene.”

Most of the tape is that scene. Hannah is newly converted: “I’ve been listening to the music for years, but actually going to Denver, going to Red Rocks, like I just did that all in 2025”. The interviewer has not heard of Red Rocks, which turns into the best passage. Duncan: “if you haven’t experienced Red Rocks yet, I envy you.” Then the explanation, geology first: “It’s like this natural amphitheater formed out of the Flatirons over in Morrison.” Two rocks form a perfect acoustic space, somebody built a venue in it, and it went famous. Hannah adds that concerts are only part of it, that “you don’t have to just go there for concerts like they do yoga you can take hikes up there”.

On the shape of the scene, Duncan hedges and then commits: “it seems like EDM is very prominent here”. Heavy bass of any kind fills a room here. “I think at one year I went to 52 shows in a year”, he says, and could have gone to more, so “I had to limit myself.” Hannah’s version of the same point is an app: “if you look up the EDM train app, you can see all the shows that go on”. Her advice to anyone who thinks they do not like the music is to go one level down, because “you have genres, which, and then you have sub-genres so there’s just like a lot of music”.

Asked for his own niche, Duncan names a subgenre called color bass and gives it a real definition: “essentially it’s like turning all of the sort of less melodic bass sounds and giving it melody”, done with computers, sometimes with vocoding. “It’s a little melodic, it’s a little heavy, which is a perfect balance for me”. He DJs under the name Archer, and says “Denver is a great place for me to be able to try and get into DJing because there’s tons of opportunities.” It started with a YouTube intro song and about ten years of rabbit hole, then a first festival, then “my brother and I got into it got some decks and just kind of taught ourselves”. The focus has since moved sideways into “learning more graphic design 3D modeling”, all under one handle: “mine is at archer.dat”.

Hannah has the graphic design degree and has drifted from it. She wants back in through charcoal, “make it like abstract and do some mixed media”. She also runs the art scene down for a visitor: the museum, and the Santa Fe Art District, “a street just full of art galleries”.

The closing question is what they would tell a kid, twenty years out. His answer: “Don’t use AI as much as you want to”, and “make sure you know how to read”. Hers: “Just be happy being a kid take one day at a time and don’t rush to grow up just live in the present”.
