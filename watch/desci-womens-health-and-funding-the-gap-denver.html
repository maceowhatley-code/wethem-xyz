DeSci, women's health, and who gets funded.

Laura Minquini, Reported by Maceo, Denver, 27 min

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: Laura Minquini, “DeSci, women's health, and who gets funded,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/desci-womens-health-and-funding-the-gap-denver. Each transcript line links to its second.

Twenty-six minutes at the DeSci Summit during ETH Denver 2024, part of a run of interviews We Them Media filmed in Colorado to document people of color in Web3. The subject is Laura Minquini, who started Athena DAO, and the conversation is about decentralized science, women’s health research, and who ends up holding the money.

She corrects the framing in her first answer. “I guess technically I’m the founder, but then because it’s a DAO, there’s a lot of other people that contribute”, she says, and names Inez Santos Silva as the second person on board.

Her route in is not the usual one. Fashion editorial, then trend forecasting for luxury groups, then her own company doing licensed fashion phone cases sold to Apple, Amazon and Neiman Marcus. Crypto arrived during the pandemic, and she came to it through longevity rather than through NFTs, learning about Vita DAO, the first bio DAO in decentralized science. Contributing there is where the problem became visible: “there’s not that many women here”, and “I realized that the funding wasn’t there, that we’re also not talking about it enough”. The conclusion she drew was not to petition anybody. It was that “we’re just going to have to create our own thing”.

The strongest stretch of the tape is her answer to the idea that women’s health has no market. She starts with the joke she is tired of, that “guys make fun of girls from aligning our chakras or crystals and incense and creams”, and then explains where that behaviour comes from: a doctor saying “sorry but there’s no way to diagnose your endometriosis other than eight operations”. The spending, she argues, is the proof rather than the punchline. “women are spending that capital. So that capital is available.” Industry claiming there is no market she calls absurd.

She is blunt about the timeline too. “it’s 2024 and women are still dying giving birth. And it disproportionately affects women of color”, and in the same breath, that “we can’t figure out how women could have children without dying”. On fertility she says “we are also not educating women into their fertility”, that “The reality is that by 35, it’s already very hard”, and that the economics compound it: “our parents owned their homes when they were 25”, while people at that age now cannot picture affording either a house or a baby.

The last third is craft. Running a DAO, she says, means “you have to think of it as coordinating multiple startups in one”, each working group with its own KPIs, and leadership that “it cannot be flat, because flat can go very wrong”. She likes the current market for it: “it’s been very good to build during the bear time because it’s truly people that are motivated that join”.

Asked about research on people of color, she goes to the data. The most complete biobank in the world is in the UK, she says, and “a lot of that genomic data doesn’t include a lot of samples from people of color”. The fix is more studies in Africa, India, Southeast Asia and Latin America, and scientists onboarded there. She puts it in one line: “You have to be intentional. That’s the biggest thing.” The rest of the tape is what intentional looks like in practice.
