Social anxiety, youth suicide and the 1%: Bobby.

Bobby, Reported by Maceo, Philadelphia, 16 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Germantown, May 2026, the question of the week: what do you think about social anxiety? Bobby is 59, from North Philadelphia, and chose to stay off camera, so the picture is Maceo walking while the conversation runs. His first answer goes straight to the youth: “it also plays a part in the suicide rate of the younger folks today,” the anxiety of “wanting to be accepted by different people,” and “no one wants to be themselves and happy with who they are.” What can a community do? “It’s real hard because you can’t monitor what the children watch,” and the young “don’t feel comfortable talking about certain things with anyone, let alone adults.”

What he would like more of: “The old conscious rappers.” On school: “The school system was never for us,” and “we have to create our own if we can, but we won’t come together as a culture or a race to help each other.” Asked how he describes himself: “I’m gonna say I’m of African descent,” born in America, and he has never done the DNA test, because “they kidnap us from our country, then we pay them for them to tell us where we’re originally from.”

His question for the city, when Maceo asks for one: “Why don’t we get along? Get along? Young against young, young against old.” Every generation says the same thing to its parents, “But We All Was The Same Age At One Time.” He remembers gang lines at Germantown Avenue and Shelton, and asks, “Why are we still living like that today?” His answer is the 1%: “If they’re controlling 99% of the population, why are we still allowing it? One, they keep everybody divided. Race, age, religion, wealth, geographical area, culture.” Two books, as he names them: The Economic Hitman and The New Jim Crow.

The last six minutes turn around. Maceo tells him crypto is “kind of like that movement re-embodied,” and Bobby, who has family in it, asks the right question: “Can somebody just steal it and you have no knowledge of where it went?” Maceo’s answer is the no-back-door argument; Bobby’s is “I can’t really speak on it. That’s something I had to learn about.” On AI Bobby sees the jobs going and the youth learning “things they shouldn’t know”; Maceo sees businesses. Bobby’s message for people around the world: “Wake up Wake up to everything around you And always be aware of your surroundings.” Names as given; Bobby’s surname was not asked.
