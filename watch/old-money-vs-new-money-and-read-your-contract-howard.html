Old money vs new money, and read your contract.

Washington, DC, 56 min, filmed 26 September 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

On Day 2 of the Black Blockchain Summit 2026, Maceo’s phone sits on a table with CJ Kelly, the certified public accountant of the two earlier pieces, in a blue blanket, Sirsu (@sirsuhayb) of BLVKHVND in a green cap, a man building a DAO, and Silas, from Benin, and the talk runs 56 minutes. Sirsu, who started Mint Fund, built a health-records app at a hackathon because every hospital he had used kept his records apart, “I moved to like 5 different places,” and won “like 10 ETH at the time, which was like maybe $80.” He started Mint Fund, “We help artists mint NFTs for free,” after a friend’s piece sold and they chose to “pay it forward.” CJ says where to hire, developers in Nairobi, Kenya, “They’re gonna work harder for a lot less,” and how he reads politics: “you have old money versus new money. It has nothing to do with Republican or Democrat.” His own position is short: “All I got to do is use Bitcoin. I’m not forcing nothing.” The old money, he says, is “trying to keep power,” and “They can’t stop it.” The table turns to Libya and Gaddafi, to whether quantum computers can break Bitcoin, and to what music feeds people: “Hearing is a form of consumption. You are whatever you consume.” From about the half hour Silas, from Benin, speaks of Benin, “it is a spiritual country,” of belief, “how God gonna believe you depend how you believe yourself,” of the five languages he speaks, and of what to do for your kids: “push him to school,” because “Writing is important,” and because “You can read your contract.” A note on the facts: Strom Thurmond joined the Republican Party in September 1964 (US Senate); Steve Jobs told Stanford’s 2005 graduates that death is very likely the single best invention of life (Stanford News); Nielsen’s 2017 US report called R&B and hip-hop the most dominant genre for the first time (Nielsen).
