Freestyle, spoken word and dance.

Reported by Maceo, Denver, 8 min, filmed 4 April 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Three performers at the Channel 5 Carnival talent show in Denver, and one piece of writing worth the whole evening: a spoken-word answer to the question of what mental health feels like for an immigrant father.

The poem opens on exhaustion, “I’ve been running on fumes, can’t run like I used to,” and a car alarm that will not stop, and turns when the speaker asks his father the question. “He says, son, restoration is a journey It’s a journey of repairs and replacements. Some parts of me have to stay, and other parts of me, they have to change. But I am still the same vehicle, and how often do you throw your vehicle away?”

Then the host’s hypotheticals. E, just the letter E, is asked which Channel 5 character he would share a studio apartment with for a year, and the story behind the animal psychic who charged $1,200 a session and sent a grieving man to strangers’ home addresses. E has no talent to declare, “but I do always love to have a good time,” so he dances.

George Raaton closes with a freestyle on topics shouted from the crowd: the gentrification of ski country by Vail Corporation, “I can’t afford to live here cause I’m living here,” mass surveillance, and the redcoats. The lo-fi beat, the handstand and most of the dancing are in the video rather than the transcript.

Transcribed from loud stage audio without the voice filter the rest of the archive uses, so the crowd and the beat are only partly on the page. Filmed from the floor at the Paramount Theatre.
