One question on a street corner after the loss.

Isaac · New York · 11 min · filmed June 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

Eleven minutes on a New York street after the Knicks lost and the series went to two to one, put together as a run of quick answers to one question: how do you keep the Knicks in 4 mentality when the Knicks are no longer in 4.

The first answers are coaching. “We gotta play hard. We gotta get back on defense.” The standard goes on the players too: “Every day coming to gym, be ready to work.”

Nobody is screened out, so the tape argues with itself. A Spurs fan is right there and says so: “it’s Spurs in 6, my heart. I’ve been jacking that since day one.” The Knicks side does not adjust the count. “We down by like 5 or 6 and we say Knicks in 4, say no more. We don’t care about the score.” Isaac, who the channel names in the title, gives his name after somebody guesses wrong: “No, it’s Isaac. I’m from New York.”

The better answers stop being about basketball. “I feel like we just got to stay strong, you know. Even though it’s a loss, and life, we all go through losses, but them losses turn into lessons.” One man turns the question into a message for anybody watching: “Perseverance is key. Whatever you going through, it gets better”, and “stay down till you come up”.

One theory keeps coming back. Donald Trump was at the game, and several people hold him responsible for it. “If he never showed up to the game, they probably would have never lost.” “He brought that badass juju to the city.” The remedy is simple enough: “Don’t let Trump come back to the Garden.”

By the last third the slogan is quietly being revised in public. “Knicks in 4 will equal Knicks in 5.” “It’s impossible, unfortunately, right now, but they can win in 5.” The basketball criticism gets specific at the same time, that “Brunson, he doesn’t have ice in his veins anymore”, that Josh Hart should sit down, and that effort by itself is not the fix. “It’s about supporting each other when y’all down, bro.”

The mentality survives the loss. What changes over eleven minutes is what people are using it for.
