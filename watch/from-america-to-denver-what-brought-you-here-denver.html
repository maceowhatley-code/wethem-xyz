A borrowed camera, and Denver block by block.

Keith · Reported by Maceo · Denver · 7 min · filmed 3 March 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

Seven minutes on a Denver street, third in a run of interviews asking people how they see the city. The camera is borrowed and being used for the first time, which Maceo says before anything else. He introduces himself, explains the plan, ten interviews before he leaves town, and gets a name: Keith.

Keith moved back about five years ago. He was working the airlines and “then I got laid off”. Before that he was a flight attendant on regional jets, “50 to 70 passengers”, running “shorter distances. But a lot of ups and downs”, out to “smaller little cities too, or towns, and then take them back to the like main hubs”.

Asked whether the job shaped how he sees America, he answers in cities. He liked Washington and could not afford it: “I lived in DC for a little while and I liked living there, but it was crazy expensive.” What he could not forgive was the transit, “the Metro didn’t go past 1 o’clock, which didn’t make no sense, and everybody closed at 2 or 3”, though he grants that “the Metro was easy to use, unlike New York City”, where asking directions gets you nowhere because “they only know their route and that’s about it”.

His Denver comes block by block. “each little area of Denver has its like little community”, he says: “up on Federal, it’s more Hispanic”, where they are standing is what “they say this is the gay part, gay community”, which he reports and then waves off, “I don’t see it, but I see a lot of gay people”, and “It’s like, who cares?” Then “down like 16th Street is like the tourist area”, currently “all under construction, so nothing’s going on”.

On politics he will not hand the city to either side. “It’s a mixture of both. Like you got conservative and liberal”, and you will find both “in the same bar”. He does not mind the governor and cannot produce the name, “Oh God, I can’t remember right now”, and the interviewer admits he could not name his own.

What he likes is the nightlife, “the entertainment, the community, some of the food”, the clubs up towards the baseball stadium, the hole in the wall bars, and the fact that “you got 3 or 4 bars in walking distance” of this one.

The last stretch is weather, and it turns into water. “the weather’s been great this winter, but no snow though”, against a last season of “constant snow”. He is not blaming the machines, “I think it’s not AI”, and puts it on El Nino pushing warm air up from the south. Water is on his mind because of fire: not in the city, he says, but on the outskirts, where a transformer arced and the wind carried it and “it torched a whole bunch of homes and everything”. Then he asks the camera for its name, and gets a booyah on a three count.
