Four months that took a Web2 developer apart.

Leo, Lagos, 5 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Supporting Patrons: Aja Davis and Donna Dalton

To quote this piece: Leo, “Four months that took a Web2 developer apart,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/broke-me-down-to-pieces-web3bridge-cohort-12-episode-5-lagos. Each transcript line links to its second.

Four and a half minutes in Ikorodu, Lagos with a developer at the end of Web3Bridge Cohort 12, the fifth episode in the run of short interviews We Them Media filmed as the cohort wrapped.

Web3Bridge founder Ayodeji Awosika opens from the same building, counting the cohort in and out and listing the countries applications for the next one came from.

The introduction is quick: “my name is Ali Onyema Joseph, popularly known as Leo. I’m from Edo State, Nigeria.” Asked for his tribe, he gives the state again.

He came in on a family recommendation. “I got to find out about Web3 Bridge from my brother”, he says, a brother who told him the new web was worth learning and that there was somewhere to learn it fast. He was already a Web2 developer, and the pull was curiosity as much as career: “I’m a man of exploration. I like to explore”, and “I’ve touched so many areas on the web, and Web3 was like still like a mystery to me”.

His case for the technology is about who gets to run things. It is “a way of decentralizing things, making things free for everybody to do things”, so that “everybody can now be their own boss”, their own server, their own computer. His verdict is short: “it’s the next wave, it’s the future.”

The sharpest passage is what the course did to his confidence. “Web3 is very, very exhilarating. It kind of makes you think about your whole stack”, he says, and then the line the tape turns on: “I came here 4 months ago thinking I was good. But then I got here and you broke me down to pieces and then built me up.” He says every developer should go through it.

The evidence he offers is his own year. “my whole experience in the Web3 space started January”, he says. “I came here not knowing anything, and right now I’ve built 2 products like in the Web3 space currently in testing, and that’s like a big win for me.”

What he wants next is a position rather than a job. He has been programming since he was younger, but “I always wanted to be part of the founding guys”, not the person “always coming back from the back”. He talks about “changing the whole scope of things”, building the knowledge base and moving the ecosystem along.

His advice to anyone thinking about applying takes one line: “just pack his bags and come. It’s a great experience.” Then the case for the building itself, made in an inventory: “Give you beds. There’s lights 24/7. There’s data.” What, he asks, are you doing at home.
