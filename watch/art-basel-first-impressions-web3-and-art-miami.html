Two first timers recap a crypto art week.

Gabrielle & Janeiro · Reported by Maceo · Miami · 38 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

A recap taped in Miami at the end of Art Basel 2023, in an Airbnb, an hour before the ride to the airport. Three people trade notes: Maceo, who runs We Them Media and came for the Web3 communities he has been part of all year, and the two people he brought with him. “I brought Gabrielle, my girlfriend, and my best friend from home in Janeiro”. Neither of them is in crypto. That is the point of the tape.

Gabrielle went in loose and came out surprised by the overlap. “I didn’t expect to meet as many people in kind of the intersection of art and the crypto world”. Janeiro went in braced for a finance conference. “I was expecting a lot of talks on numbers and the uses of a lot of vocabulary that I’ve yet to learn yet in this crypto space”.

Both of them expected the rooms to look one way and found them looking another. “Something I didn’t expect was definitely the inclusivity of all the spaces that we went to”, Gabrielle says, still predominantly white but with far more women and more people who looked like them than she had pictured. Janeiro puts the same finding his own way: “So it wasn’t just a bunch of old men in glasses with pocket protectors.”

The highlights are ordinary and specific. Janeiro liked the digital art rave best, and how “they somehow finessed an art museum, a low-key art museum into a rave”. Gabrielle liked meeting people she had only heard named at home: “a highlight for me was really putting faces to names”. Her most useful conversation was at the first event, straight off the plane, with a man called Sage who is paid entirely in crypto. She had not known that was a thing anyone did: “to meet someone that is really having that experience and able to travel the world as well through being paid entirely through crypto”.

The best answer on the tape is Janeiro’s, on what he is taking home. He had been braced to be treated as a nobody, and was not. “some of these crypto people are still focused on the new guy, the person that just got their foot in the door”. He turns it into a demand on the space: “you need people who are thinking about the little guy Thinking about the person just trying to get in the door”. Later he relays the same thought back from someone who works on access, Lindsay: “she said, you gotta sort of get it simple enough for people to be able to use it”.

Gabrielle is thinking about her own field. She wants to know where Web3 sits in medicine and public health, and says she is “looking forward to possibly finding my own, like, community within that setting to find out more information about my interest in medicine and crypto on Web3”.

Asked why the three of them being there mattered, Janeiro answers first and shortest: “Because the work matters, and so does the goal, the pursuit, the journey itself”. Gabrielle answers as the person who made the shirts they are wearing, and frames it as proof of a team: “I think the whole point of the brand is to be inclusive and community work.”

Maceo makes his own case last, about showing up in a bear market when other people have stopped. “I’m definitely not interested in, you know, just showing up to capitalize but showing up to contribute.” And on why he keeps bringing his own people into rooms like these: “I think it’s important that the visions of people that look like us come to life in this technology”.

They also lost their Airbnb on arrival, six hours into carrying their bags, five minutes before check in, and found another one by evening. He rates the trip an eight.
