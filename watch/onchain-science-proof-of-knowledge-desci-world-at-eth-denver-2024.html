Onchain science, proof of knowledge: DeSci World.

Jelani Clarke and Josh Bates, Reported by Maceo, Denver, 30 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: Jelani Clarke and Josh Bates, “Onchain science, proof of knowledge: DeSci World,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/onchain-science-proof-of-knowledge-desci-world-at-eth-denver-2024. Each transcript line links to its second.

Thirty minutes at the DeSci Summit on the University of Colorado Denver campus, the last day of ETH Denver 2024, with the two people running it. Maceo got there by chance: “somebody talked to my girlfriend, was like, oh, you’re in clinical research? You gotta come.” Dr. Jelani Clarke and Josh Bates of DeSci World explain what they built. The summit “has been being planned since, I think, October,” as “a democratic organization” that delegates to the community, “which is both sometimes great and other times not so great.” Five days of events, and at the same time a hacker house where “Jelani, myself, and 22 other people have actually been hacking” on “what we’re calling the interoperable DeSci stack.”

How each got here. Josh: “I was living in China for a number of years and I wanted to send some money home to my family. So I used crypto to do so,” then full-time crypto in 2020 and DeSci World in 2021. Jelani has “a PhD in neuroimmunology,” worked in biotech, and left academia over “the bureaucracy and the red tape.” His comparison is to the music business: “Scientists in academia predominantly operate under a 360 deal type architecture where you don’t own much of what you publish.” So he “just looked up blockchain and DeSci, blockchain and science, and that led me down the rabbit hole.”

On money, Josh is blunt. “People are not altruistic,” so DeSci builds incentives around giving. He bootstrapped the project himself: “I’m lucky enough to have made some good trades during the bull market of 2020,” and his advice to anyone raising is to “crowdsource from people with aligned values rather than through sort of VCs.” Jelani lays out the pillars beyond funding: “frameworks of ownership, data management, access, publication,” and the 23andMe example, where you sign your genetics away and “someone else can’t go and sell it for a profit” under the structures they are building. Is DeSci science on the blockchain? “Josh said yes, I say no.” It is a product of the open science movement: “a progression of science back to its original state, where anybody who wants to participate” can.

The history: “I was here three years ago to the day,” in “the DCI house,” where people who have since raised millions first met. Then the present, “the adoption phase,” their podcast DeSci Rising, and the hacker house workshops, twenty of them, where “it’s like, now I’m going to share what I’m doing” instead of pitching. Josh describes Proof of Knowledge, “a unified data layer That allows you to create knowledge and attest to it on-chain,” where “When your knowledge is utilized by an LLM, you’re paid for the utilization,” and the partnership with Lemonade Social. Jelani sees two paradigms, grassroots DeSci and enterprise DeSci integrating with “the Pfizer and the universities and the biobanks,” and says of the second, “that’s really what’s going to drive adoption.”

The year ahead: DeSci.World V2, Oxford, seven countries in Asia, “staying in Zanzibar for an entire month, trialing proof of knowledge as a coordination framework for a network state.” Josh’s closing: “Desai is just as much a cultural movement as it is a technological movement.” And Jelani’s, to the camera: “we need you as much as you need us,” whether you are a neuroimmunologist or “a degen.” Maceo admits it is only his second formal interview of the week. Josh: “If this is your second interview, then We Them Media is going to be big.” Language is unfiltered; the show intro uses its earlier name. The transcript below is from the original audio.
