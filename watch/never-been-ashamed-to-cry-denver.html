What is the most important global issue? Eric, 56, on a Denver street, says harmony, and getting there takes twenty-four minutes and every funeral he has been to.

He is from Shreveport, Louisiana. His mother died at fifty-seven. The centre of it is his grandmother: he came home from the state wrestling championship at eleven, happy with how he had done, and she had died. His father told him he was getting to the age where he did not have to be carried past this kind of thing any more.

At the funeral: “I was the last one up there to see her. And I looked down and I knew I’m never going to see this woman again and I started crying and I felt good. And I’ve been crying ever since.” He returns to it: “I’ve never been ashamed to cry in my life. I get it out. It’s over with, it’s done, and I don’t worry about it no more.”
