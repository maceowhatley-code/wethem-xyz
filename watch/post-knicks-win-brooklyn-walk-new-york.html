A walk through Brooklyn on the night they won.

New York, 9 min · filmed June 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

To quote this piece: “A walk through Brooklyn on the night they won,” We Them Media, filmed 2026-06, https://wethem.xyz/watch/post-knicks-win-brooklyn-walk-new-york. Each transcript line links to its second.

Nine minutes of walking and asking on a Brooklyn night, filmed the evening the Knicks won. There is no sit down interview here. One person with a camera goes street by street with the same two questions: how are we feeling about this Knicks win, and what does this mean for the city.

The fullest answer arrives early and comes out as a list of names. “Oh, bro, it means Sprewell. It means Alonzo Mourning.” From there it is years of people calling the Knicks losers, people who switched to the Nets, and the fact that “I haven’t seen a Nets jersey in forever.” The filmer’s own summary is shorter: “New York been in their slump. Knicks just changed the script.”

Midway through, a woman stops him on the sidewalk to ask for money. He offers to buy her food at the chicken spot he is heading to, she says it is too far, and he lets it go without making a scene of her: “But you know, I’m not really here to put people on blast. It’s not that deep. Life is hard.” That leads straight into why he is out at all: “That’s why we’re turning on a camera and just creating value, because the world needs to know this isn’t just any moment, man.”

Consent comes up on its own. “You shooting a video?” somebody asks, and the answer on the tape is “I’m trying to get better at telling people in advance instead of instead of filming them.” Two people take the offer and use it. “Congratulations, New York City.” Asked what the night means, one of them says “It means everything to the city”, and when the filmer pushes on why, gives him the whole season: “Everything. We turning up the whole summer.”

Late on, two young women give the reason plainly, that “it’s been so long since they ever like, um, won a game and won the championship”, and that it will move money: “Probably gonna make fans like millions of dollars.”

He finishes talking to the camera, walking, having admitted he is not a New Yorker. The last thing he says is not about basketball at all. “I think you needed to win, bro. Your work is worth something.” Then he tells the city to turn the camera on and get active, and goes to find something to eat.
