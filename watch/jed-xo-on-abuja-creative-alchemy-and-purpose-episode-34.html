JED XO on Abuja, Creative Alchemy and purpose.

JED XO, Reported by Maceo, Abuja, 31 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

Episode 34, recorded in Abuja at the end of Maceo’s first trip to Nigeria, the summer of 2024, with the man who hosted him. Maceo’s framing: “I traveled to Africa for the first time, specifically to Lagos and Abuja, Nigeria,” to connect with community members and to deepen “our partnership with Creative Alchemy, a community deeply aligned with our mission to bring artists, Nigerian communities, and African communities broadly on-chain.” JED XO met him at the door with a Central Cee song on loop, then for two weeks made sure “That I’ve been fed That I’ve been presentable, per se, even And that I’ve been conscientious about where I take my camera and how I use it.”

JED on the fortnight: “amazing is the one word to sum it all up,” and a shift “like mentally,” from being around creatives and “out of my cubicle.” He had never received a guest. What Maceo showed him about his own country: the inflation, seen from outside, and the idea of taking the bus to Abuja instead of a plane, “it’s something that even I that live here never tried before.” The trip he most wanted Maceo to make was Badagry, “the whole slave museum,” because “that has a lot to do with your heritage, your origin as a black man.” And “if you don’t know your history, it’s going to be a lot difficult to, to make a change.”

The Ikorodu day, by speedboat, to Web3Bridge, is the turning point of the episode. “Someone cares enough to want to educate other people,” JED says of Ayodeji, who is “empowering a lot of youths that would have been maybe on the streets committing crimes.” The lesson he took: “We as Nigerians often times address the problems that we face more than even talking about possible solutions.” Maceo adds that he cannot name ten Black founders in the space, “let alone 10 people working on trying to build schools to bring more black people into the space,” and that Ethereum Foundation backing for the hub “says a lot about, like, the Web3 ecosystem.”

What JED wants to do with it: “the best weapon that I have at my disposal right now is my voice, is my music,” and “I want to try to like, you know, make conscious music.” His name, Ebenezer, “means a stone of help,” so “I want to build like food banks where people can just wake up and Go get food and eat without having to pay anything.” On Web3 he calls himself an explorer who has been through NFTs, memes and DeFi, and wants back to his music community, “that side of DeFi is is still very much like a toddler.” Platforms, in order: Manifold, “that’s how I minted my first song ever,” then Decent and Sound, a song on Cardano through a team that walked him through it, and Vault on Solana next. Creative Alchemy gave him his first panel, unplanned, and “we are creative alchemy as well.”

The last third is the house: Dan, “he’s been our dad on this trip,” and PressTK, the producer JED had only ever worked with over sent beats, now in the room, “he runs with it and even does it better than how I imagine it to come out.” Maceo closes with why the podcast exists: “I started this podcast knowing I wanted to find a community of creators I could create with And it’s crazy to say that here I am in West Africa and I found that community because of this podcast.” And on the country: “I haven’t felt unsafe once since I arrived here.” Language is unfiltered; the show intro uses its earlier name.
