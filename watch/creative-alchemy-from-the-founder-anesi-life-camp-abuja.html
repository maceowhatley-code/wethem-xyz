Creative Alchemy, from the founder: Ánesi at Life Camp.

Ánesi · Abuja · 38 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

The full sit-down with Ánesi, founder of Creative Alchemy, at Life Camp in Abuja during Maceo’s first trip to Nigeria. The one-minute reel on this site was cut from it. Maceo’s framing at the top: the trip is about “deepening our partnership with Creative Alchemy, a community deeply aligned with our mission to bring artists, Nigerian communities and African communities broadly on chain.” She is introduced as “a founder of Creative Alchemy, a performing artist, a former football player, former dancer, a person of many lives.”

Why she built it. Music school, an a cappella band, a manager, and then a festival in Abuja: “I felt music was disrespected, or creativity was disrespected and I told myself that that was going to be the last the last time I was going to go for any events like that.” The decision: “It is time we start creating the experiences. It is time we start making the change and difference by ourselves.” What it became: “It’s been the home that I’ve always wanted, that I always wish I had and it’s been a home for others.”

Her definition is wide, “anybody that can solve a problem is a creative, a developer, a graphic designer, a shoemaker, a fashion designer, a poet,” and the point of community is “not feel alone while going through this whole journey.” The resources she wants: a hub, a studio, a visual studio, a VR studio, “where you can just come be yourself.” And the thing that bothers her about the space: “more importance placed on the developers and some other set of people in the Web3 aside creatives.”

Maceo answers with his own start. “I got into the crypto space because my roommate told me to buy an ENS domain. I got like an airdrop and I was like, I’m gonna start my podcast. But I didn’t see any black people really like doing crypto journalism at all.” His way in was never finance: “the everyday person can learn about it through culture.” On music NFTs he is blunt about the model, “But we shouldn’t be just selling NFTs in the first place,” and about the platforms, “most of these platforms are just, like, white people who’ve got money to build a platform.” What brought him to Nigeria was community, “seeing Dan, my most active community member,” and what he found at Creative Alchemy was a level of care he had not worked with: “is the angle correct is the lighting correct.”

How she got into Web3: through AI first, a year of prompt engineering, then a four-day hackathon on the NEAR blockchain, chosen over a one-year creative programme, won with a Telegram bot, and what convinced her was how the prize was paid: “The actual decentralized process on how we actually verified and got our funds was what marveled me.” Then SheFi, whose classes “kept a routine of a subconscious always trying to learn,” and a first NFT that was her own song, Trapiano. The goal for Creative Alchemy: “educating one billion creative,” because “creativity is a universal language.” Maceo: “I’m excited to see how I can contribute and bring creative alchemy to Philadelphia.”

The last third is personal. The record deal she declined, “$25 per month for five, 5 years,” her fourth label offer refused. Her father’s death in January 2023, then her producer’s; selling her phone to stay off the internet; nearly not releasing Trapiano: “if I don’t do this, I don’t know who’s going to do it.” What she protects: “the most important thing to me is just expression and I protect that like a treasury.” On Afrobeats: “A lot of people will tell you that you can’t sell if you don’t make Afro beats. And that constrained mindset is what I don’t let my mind get entangled with.” The future of her music: “Diversity, actually.” Her last words for the creatives out there: “keep building in public,” and “don’t feel like you’re the only weirder, bro, because we are weirders together.”

Filmed and produced by the Creative Alchemy team. Executive producer: C.Y. Lee. Supported by Donna Dalton and Aja Davis. The transcript below is from the original audio.
