The beats behind Ready to Die, in full: Easy Mo Bee at Black and Nobel.

Philadelphia, 27 min, filmed 11 October 2026

To quote this piece: “The beats behind Ready to Die, in full: Easy Mo Bee at Black and Nobel,” We Them Media, filmed 2026-10-11, https://wethem.xyz/watch/making-the-beats-behind-ready-to-die-in-full-philadelphia. Each transcript line links to its second.

How long did it take to make Ready to Die? Easy Mo Bee, signing his book at Black and Nobel on South Street in Philadelphia, says: “So it’s like 6 songs. The most songs you’re on it? On Ready to Die? I did 6 songs.” Lion’s share, he says, is where he learned the word. One sample did not get cleared: “Because of that, it had to get pulled off.” He brought the first original pressing: “the album I got still got all the samples.”

On Busta Rhymes, Everything Remains Raw: “it must have been anywhere from probably about 12 to 15 tracks on the board.” He drove out to Long Island to record it, and says he is “real picky in particular about my sound.”

On how long a beat takes: “Flavor in Your Ear, I remember I made that probably like 20, 25 minutes.” Others take two weeks. His rule: “stay away from the beat like 2 or 3 days, come back 3, 3 days later with fresh ears.” And: “Don’t force it.”

The Pink Cookies story: Matty C of the Source’s Unsigned Hype jumps in his car on Myrtle Avenue, and he explains how a sample at a blues tempo had to be chopped. “I was one of the first to do that.”

Why the book: “Just long as people get to learn some stuff about me that they probably never knew.” Denzel Garrick signs off at the end.

Filmed 11 October 2026 at Black and Nobel in Philadelphia.
