Where the name came from: Maceo, interviewed by Archive.

Maceo, Reported by Archive, New York, 17 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

New York, the start of 2024, the earliest account of the podcast on tape. Blue, of Archive, a zine from Blue Calf Magazine, asks the questions. Where did the name come from? A line heard in high school, and then years of thinking about community: the name was “a way to build a brand that kind of stands in that same power and pride.” What it is for: “how do we build something that works and supports everyone in the margins?”

The dates: “I did my first episode probably in like maybe March or April 2022, like right before my senior year of college.” The plan then was to interview producers, as a rapper; the realisation, “how do I actually add value?” The audience, honestly: “I feel like the podcast probably has like less than 50 listeners.” So he builds for later: “what I’m trying to build is build for people when they are interested again,” and “how can I be someone that looks like me that’s answering those questions as opposed to someone that doesn’t look like me?” He is not a finance person; he holds little crypto and sees the NFT space as “creating an artist middle class.”

The standout so far is the first in-person interview, at Rec Philly. The direction is already the one this site took two years later: “how do we pivot from being a MusicWeb3 podcast To a community-driven, human rights-oriented publication.” And the line for the whole period: “this is practice, this is not the product.”

On anime he names Luffy and G Herbo and bell hooks as role models and reads One Piece as a story about injustice taking different shapes in different places. On method: “I’m really interested in doing a profile on people,” from “who are you” to “what’s important to you” to why, which takes patience. The feedback: his great aunt said the name has to change, and his answer, “it’s about demanding dignity and recognition for our humanity, not sidestepping, you know, what we already identify with to ask for that.” Why the podcast exists: “there’s really, like, stifled discourse in the black community and impoverished communities in a lot of ways. You know, we don’t have safe spaces.”

Last, advice to himself at thirteen, at boarding school with time to kill: “think about what you’re excited to learn about, um, and use the internet as a resource to do that,” find the people writing about it, and message them. Blue’s summary: “less Fortnite, more conversations.” Language is unfiltered; the show’s name is said in full throughout. The transcript below is from the original audio.
