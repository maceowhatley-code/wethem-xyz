ETHConf: ETH as digital oil, and a school anyone can teach at.

Han, New York, 10 min · filmed June 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

A co-founder of two blockchain companies, met at ETHConf in New York, starts with what he calls a forgotten use case: “We feel like there’s a forgotten use case in tokenizing ownership in companies and smart contractifying them.” His example is two people who own a company half and half and program their tokens so that neither can sell to a stranger: “I don’t want to have like suddenly your uncle come in.” What people miss, he says, is that “it’s the ability to make equity and ownership programmable that is very misunderstood still”.

His picture of ETH is fuel for an economy: “If you want to think about BTC as a digital gold, then think about ETH as a digital oil.” He expects the price swings to settle as real-world assets move on-chain, and he sees Ethereum as the layer that AI agents will pay each other on.

Asked whether AI will undermine people working in teams, he goes the other way: “It’s not gonna undermine it. It’s gonna actually upgrade humanity.” Because “Understanding can’t be prompted.” The talk drifts into consciousness, and he admits “We’re going into very deep territory here”. It ends on the project closest to him, a nonprofit school where “anybody who feels like they want to transfer knowledge can transfer it by way of a lecture, a course.” “It’s called Dedu, decentralized education.”
