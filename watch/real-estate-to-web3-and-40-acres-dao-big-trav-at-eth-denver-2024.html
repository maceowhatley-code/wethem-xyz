Real estate to Web3, and 40 Acres DAO: Big Trav.

Big Trav, Reported by Maceo, Denver, 11 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: Big Trav, “Real estate to Web3, and 40 Acres DAO: Big Trav,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/real-estate-to-web3-and-40-acres-dao-big-trav-at-eth-denver-2024. Each transcript line links to its second.

ETH Denver 2024, in the series FWB paid for. Maceo opens by saying so: “Shout out to FWB, everybody who voted for me to come out here and work on this project.” Across from him is Big Trav, founder of W3bbie, at his first ETH Denver: “lived up to the hype, I will say.” What he likes most is seeing people again, the ones he stayed with at a hacker house in Paris, and “being with my boys Russ and Dave.” Maceo remembers meeting Russ the year before, walking around “trying to meet black people,” and being told “you got to meet 40 acres they’re the community,” and now being back at the event Russ organised.

How Trav got in is the surprise of the piece. “real estate brought me into Web3 space.” Someone who knew his real estate development work was moving into “physical locations backed by NFTs” and wanted him on the team. “three, four years later, here I am now.” Before that, a few rental properties, then the pandemic and Clubhouse, “I got to meet like a crazy network that I probably wouldn’t have gotten any other way.”

On his own progress he is unbothered by the pivots: “As I always say, I have past lives I like to evolve, I like to be, you know, water.” The core memory is a hackathon. “I’d never done a hackathon. I did it for my 30th birthday,” three days with two friends at an ETH Global and Zora hackathon, “we were top eight finalists,” second place from Polygon. It told him W3bbie should build products, not only collectibles.

W3bbie, in his words: “we are rebranding Web3,” making it “make more sense culturally, especially for our communities,” and “we’re really just a collective of builders of color.” The first project, Found on Mars, was Midjourney art, 3,333 pieces, dropped free with no marketing. “I thought that would take a couple weeks maybe a month it happened in an hour.” Then the secondary market made money for the collectors: “we’ve made two and a half ETH for people off something that we put out for free.” The product he uses to explain utility is the Nasty Pass, a fast pass for DJ Mike Nasty’s New York parties with rarity levels that let you skip the line and bring “a plus one to a plus four.”

At the end Trav turns it around and congratulates Maceo on the FWB proposal: “Governance is crazy,” and “a lot of people get stuff passed and get the funds and then they disappear.” Language is unfiltered; the show intro uses its earlier name. The transcript below is from the original audio.
