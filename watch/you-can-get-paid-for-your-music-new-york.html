An artist on music on-chain and a platform closing.

Dora, New York, 8 min · filmed June 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Eight minutes in New York with a songwriter who records under the name Hashdrowned, mostly about what happens to music platforms when the money runs out. It opens with the question the channel puts to people in crypto, and the answer comes back with no pause at all: “Don’t fuck this up. Be good, I guess, if you’re going to get rich.”

Then the introduction. She is Dora, and she records as Hashdrowned. “I’m an artist, songwriter, and I write and record alternative anti-vogue Americana music.” She lists where the music lives, Instagram, Spotify, SoundCloud, and Nina, and then stops on Nina, because it is closing. “Yeah, but they’re getting shut down. They ran out of money. It’s really sad.” The interviewer says he met the founders of that project. She thinks the thing itself worked, “It was working for a long time”, and that what it lacked was money coming back in: “it just needs more ways for it to generate.”

Her read on why it did not last is the best part of the tape, and it is not about technology. “I think a lot of the traction was more so musicians getting excited to upload to it, and then not as many people being excited to find it.” She uploaded her own music there after watching friends do it, and she describes the economics plainly: when you do pay, “they add like a dollar to take or something like that, and then the rest goes to the artist. So I think it’s like a paid Spotify.”

From there it becomes an argument about who should have bought it. She floats Bandcamp; the interviewer argues for SoundCloud and for the Patreon model, and says the audience is already there, “There’s people who already spend money, right, on Bandcamp.” She is not sold, because “there are a lot of people that don’t understand crypto or the blockchain.”

Which turns the interview around. She asks the question the video is named for: “how does it make sense to, like, put a song on the blockchain?” The answer she gets is busking, and then a walk through wallets and how the money lands: “So then that money, how does it get to the artist?”

The last stretch is work. She is staying up for a day as a movie extra, booked through Central Casting, “You just sign up on there, and then it’s a bit of a process, but it’s free”, and the day is standing around in the right clothes with a tattoo covered. It ends on the plug, and her name spelled out.
