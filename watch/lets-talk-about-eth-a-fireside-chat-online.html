A hack, a rollback and a round on weaknesses.

Reported by Maceo, Online, 52 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

To quote this piece: “A hack, a rollback and a round on weaknesses,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/lets-talk-about-eth-a-fireside-chat-online. Each transcript line links to its second.

Fifty two minutes of a three way livestream, the second fireside chat on Let’s Talk About Eth, with Maceo and his two co-hosts, Zaal and Ohnahji. Maceo lost at nose goes, so he runs the intro: “We played nose goes on who run the intro, and I, I didn’t win.” He is home in Philadelphia, and moves to the steps when his connection drops.

It opens on life news. One co-host has two sons who just turned eighteen: “2 of my boys, they turned 18.” Maceo calls it eighteen years of showing up as a Black dad, and the answer is about who gets counted: “media won’t highlight that type vibes, but it’s a lot of them.” The other co-host got engaged.

Then Maceo in the hot seat. He is at his parents’ house with his sister, her two kids, four dogs and a cat. Six weeks in Colorado gave him a format he likes, and he does not pretend it comes naturally. “I just see somebody and I feel called to interview them and I just say, hey, can I interview you for my YouTube channel?” Then “they look at me a little bit crazy most of the time”. He has shot two or three street interviews in Philadelphia already, on the mayor.

The summer goal is local: “how do I get Philadelphia behind my content this summer?” Around it, an application to the Boys and Girls Club, a barista shift at a cafe, a weekly creator call a friend hosts. He wants other people making the work because they want to: “I’m just falling in love with the idea of people enjoying doing what I’m doing”. A co-host reads an older line back at him, “I call myself a media company, but I’m not putting out that much media”, and says it has stopped being true.

The news half opens on a hack: fake money minted, traded through Aave, people unable to withdraw. “the hacks aren’t new. We’re gonna see more, unfortunately”, one co-host says. What shook the timeline was Arbitrum’s security council voting to take roughly seventy million dollars back off the attacker. “A lot of the space was like, that’s not decentralization.” The counter: “If you don’t like it, you don’t have to play in that environment.” Maceo asks a better question, “is decentralization just about technology or is it really about decision-making?”, then answers it against himself: “if I got my money back, I wouldn’t be pissed.”

Vercel and Lovable had breaches the same week, and he stops for the audience he actually wants. “For my mom who doesn’t know that, but maybe will watch this, what is a Vercel?” The screen share after it is a token tool with a treasury, leaderboards and a raffle. “I don’t know anything about anything you’re saying right now.”

The last stretch is the best of it: each names a weakness. Scheduling, says one. For the other, “tribal knowledge in my head that isn’t actually explained out for someone to capture”. Maceo goes longest. Two years ago he was late to five of every seven commitments a day, now one or two. He books calls back to back, tells himself he will do it later, and then: “I feel guilty and ashamed and overwhelmed and angry. And I just want to close my laptop and go on a walk and feel inspired.”

Money is where he lands. Months in Africa changed how he thinks about paying people, and having no income himself puts him nearer level with his team than when a patron funded him. Twenty dollars sent on a weekend is not arbitrary, he says: it is that times a number of people. What he wants is months of overhead so his team has job security: “how do I honor my teammates without feeling like I’m exploiting them?” What he is after is a team worth funding rather than one worth speculating on.
