A hitchhiker on a spiritual mission.

Reported by Maceo · Denver · 11 min · filmed 4 April 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Alex is sitting outside the Channel 5 venue in Denver without a ticket. Born in Texas, a year in Massachusetts, then, as he puts it, his life fell apart and he started hitchhiking across the country. Five weeks on the road, a week in Denver, and a conversation that gets to God, homelessness, and the man who held a knife to him for six hours.

He is a fan from a distance: “I saw Andrew Colligan’s show and I was like, damn, I want to go there, but I can’t.” What he likes is who gets interviewed. “I think it’s cool how he interviews like homeless people and just like regular people you don’t see that a lot with other interviewers or YouTubers.”

The story America is not covering, he says, is spiritual. “God is real.” He works at it out loud, and gets to this: “people who have found God are able to influence the world in certain ways,” for good and for harm. His question for the world is the plainest one in the archive: “why do we treat our homeless like they’re filth, why do we treat each other like we hate each other, why don’t we just love each other.”

On being broke: “It’s extremely challenging.” He chose it, he says, to grow faster, and it has: “It’s actually Vastly increased the pace in which I grow spiritually.” The route was Massachusetts, Burlington, New York, Virginia, North Carolina, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Colorado. The hardest moment: “one guy held a knife to me for six hours.”

The interviewer offers the 2-1-1 line and a call to a friend who does housing activism. Alex’s message for Andrew: “I’m a man of the people. Come and find me, please.” Filmed by Maceo with Tim Palka on camera.
