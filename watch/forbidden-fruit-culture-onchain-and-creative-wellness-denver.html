A Philly artist on culture, gas fees and Forbidden Fruit.

Stonez the Organic, Reported by Maceo, Denver, 11 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: Stonez the Organic, “A Philly artist on culture, gas fees and Forbidden Fruit,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/forbidden-fruit-culture-onchain-and-creative-wellness-denver. Each transcript line links to its second.

Ten minutes at ETH Denver in 2024, in the middle of an evening party, with a Philadelphia artist and community builder who has been at the conference since Tuesday and has lost his voice for it. Stonez the Organic runs the Forbidden Fruit events in Philly with Ty, and most of this conversation is about what the technology owes the people making things with it.

What has him excited at this particular conference is not a chain or a product. “the thing that’s exciting me the most right now is the attention to culture”, he says. “People are really paying attention to the movers and people who are contributing more than just the tech to the space.” His argument for why that matters is practical rather than sentimental: “the more that we value culture, the more enticing and inclusive the space becomes.”

Pressed on whether he came into crypto expecting culture to be part of it, he says he studied culture in school and thinks it is part of everything. Without it, “there becomes a lack of realness, a lack of authenticity”, and then the only shared reason to do anything is money. What he found instead when he arrived was “a lack of diversity, a lack of differentiated opinions”, and he is blunt about where that leads: “when you have an echo chamber in that type of situation, it can only be good for so long”.

He came in as a collector before he came in as an artist. “I spent a lot of my time collecting art, just getting to know people before I even started putting my own out there.” What kept him was finding people who wanted the technology to do something, which he puts as “liberating the world from a lot of the traumas and constructs that we just became okay with”.

Forbidden Fruit predates all of it. “We’ve been doing Forbidden Fruit since 2019”, he says, and the work now is to “create a bridge for artists who are not necessarily crypto native to be introduced to the tech”.

The advice section is the most useful part of the tape, and it is unglamorous. “I would say do as much research as possible and take your time.” Then the line worth printing on something: “If you’re an artist, don’t come in this space and try to become a trader. You’re not a trader.” Learn to trade if you want to learn to trade, he says, but first “figure out how the tech works for you”.

On what he wants next from the tech, he wants the friction gone. He is watching the Superchain, which he explains as Optimism, Zora and Base working together, and what he likes about this year is that “I don’t have to log into my MetaMask or worry about a seed phrase”. Fees are not an abstraction to him: “A gas fee being over ten dollars that could be someone’s transportation”. His summary: “Once the tech becomes manageable and digestible for everyone, then we’ll be in a very good place”, though he immediately takes the word back and says what he means is inclusive.

Asked for a core memory, he picks meeting online friends in person for the first time, in Atlanta, and laughs at the warning he grew up with about strangers on the internet: “it was just really dope to see like people of color come together”. Asked what is coming next from Forbidden Fruit: “just follow us, I can’t talk about that”.
