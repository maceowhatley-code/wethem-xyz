The Philly barbershop interview.

Reported by Maceo, Philadelphia, 29 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Why does a barber’s licence take 1,250 hours? A haircut in Germantown, Philadelphia, turns into a half hour on what a barber has to know.

He is working toward a Pennsylvania barber teacher’s licence, and what he wants to teach is not cutting: “Health and safety. That’s the main reason for barbering.” A borrowed cup, clippers that need disinfecting, a dirty blade on a neck: “It’s a lot of stuff that you would take for granted, that you should learn about if you’re trying to cut corners on cutting hair.”

He calls it barber law, like a driving licence: the licence proves you practise it. The hours are 1,250, about nine months, or 555 if you are already a cosmetologist.

Asked who Maceo’s content is for, Maceo says it is “an archive of Philadelphia,” with teams doing the same for their own communities, so “our brand becomes like the BBC, but your local broadcaster.” The barber’s advice: go a little political, because that is how you get a community rather than a crowd. His last line: “You made me a customer. Now I got two barbers in Germantown.”
