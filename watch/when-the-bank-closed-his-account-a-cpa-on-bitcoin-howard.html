When the bank closed his account: a CPA on Bitcoin.

Washington, DC · 14 min · filmed 26 September 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

On Day 2 of the Black Blockchain Summit 2026, Maceo talks with Charles J. Kelly, “also known as CJ the Smart Guy,” a certified public accountant who has spoken at the summit for years. He spoke that day on the rules for stablecoins, among them the tax rules, which he calls absurd for making people report “every time you sell, spend, or trade”; his view, not tax advice. Then the story that made Bitcoin real for him: in LA, on his way to mail his CPA application, his card was declined and the bank said “hey, we closed your account.” Bitcoin on Coinbase and a roommate got him through, though he still slept at the airport. “I realized, wow, to be outside the system is revolutionary,” he says. For Kamal’s question he calls DeFi “huge” and defines it plainly; for inflation he uses peach cobbler: “inflation is an influx of anything.” His One Question starts with “are you happy working for money that goes down in value?” and his message is Nipsey Hussle’s: “find your purpose or you’re wasting air.” A note on the facts: the IRS does require stablecoin transactions to be reported (IRS); the money supply grew 51 percent and prices 30 percent from January 2020 to August 2026 (Federal Reserve, BLS); at the Central Bank of Kenya’s 129.67 shillings to the dollar, 90 million shillings is about $694,000, not $70,000, which is about 9 million; bitcoin’s record so far is about $126,000, set in October 2025 (Kraken); the $1 billion royalty figure he gives for a DeFi developer’s patents is his own calculation, which we could not confirm.
