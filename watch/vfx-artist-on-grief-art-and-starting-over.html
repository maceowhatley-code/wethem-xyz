Kale, a VFX artist, on grief, her mom Sarah, and working on Marvel films.

Kale, Reported by Maceo, Denver, 29 min · filmed 27 March 2026

A Denver stop-motion and visual effects artist on making art after losing her mother, walking away from a Hollywood VFX job, and what release means when creating is the only thing that helps.

Kale spent three years at a visual effects studio with credits on Mission: Impossible and Marvel films, and stopped making anything of her own. “I would put all my creative energy and juices in doing what the client, the director, whomever told me what to do, and I’d get home and I’d have no energy, no motivation to create anything for myself.”

Her mother Sarah died two years ago, after a long struggle with mental illness and addiction. “She was such a bright light in such a dark room,” Kale says. “She loved those connections with people, genuinely getting to know people. Not listening to your binary answers, but listening to your story.” On the diagnoses her mother kept looking for: “Nothing is wrong with you. You just have a different brain than everybody else. We all have different brains.”

On finding her way back to making things, at a festival in Buena Vista, Colorado, on her own for the first time: “I hadn’t created anything at this point for so long. Then the second day I woke up and it was such a bright day, not a cloud in the sky, and I felt so alive.” She filled a sketchbook in one sitting. When somebody walked past and told her she should sell it: “I hear you, but this is for me right now. This is not for monetary value.”

And on conversation itself, which is most of what this interview turns out to be about: “It’s the what and how questions that are really the thought-provoking questions. Why are you feeling this way? That will create stories and a genuine connection.”

This conversation includes discussion of suicide, mental illness and addiction.
