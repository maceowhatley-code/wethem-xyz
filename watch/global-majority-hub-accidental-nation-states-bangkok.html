Global Majority Hub: accidental nation-states, and what comes next.

Bangkok, 10 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: “Global Majority Hub: accidental nation-states, and what comes next,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/global-majority-hub-accidental-nation-states-bangkok. Each transcript line links to its second.

A discussion at the Global Majority Hub in Bangkok, picked up near its end, about what people should belong to first if the systems they grew up under are coming apart. One participant puts the question: if “the current regime is collapsing and maybe crypto is like slightly like filling the kind of gaps”, why do we still introduce ourselves by country?

A speaker from Malaysia answers with history. The book The Network State asks a question worth keeping, whether a community-first entity can exist, and separately offers one answer to it. For most of the global majority, he says, the nation-state came late: “most of GM, the global majority, are kind of like accidental nation states that kind of emerged out of independence.” Before that came the company state: “All of Congo was basically owned by a company for the King of Belgium, right?” Nations were then built on purpose, with a language, a curriculum and a song, the imagined communities Benedict Anderson wrote about.

Others push further. “Is anybody here thinking about bioregions?” Because “the nation-state is not adapted to take care of the ecology.” A planner suggests functional rooms instead of state lines, and the history gets its bookend: “So that was Bandung in the 1950s, maybe it’s Bangkok in 2024.” Asked how far off any of this is, the close is about the pace of collapse: “if your banking system collapses, it doesn’t matter whether you like crypto or not, you’re going to look for alternatives.”

Filmed in Bangkok in November 2024. The transcript below is from the original audio.
