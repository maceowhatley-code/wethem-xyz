Bonnie, from Aurora, on fentanyl at Purple Haze Denver.

Bonnie · Reported by Maceo · Denver · 10 min · filmed 27 March 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Asked for the most important issue in the world, a man outside a Denver head shop says fentanyl, tells a story that explains why, and then changes his mind on camera. The answer he lands on is the reason to watch it.

Bonnie is from Aurora. His first answer comes with a scene attached. He is in sober living, and a man turned up to group high, fell over and broke his neck. His frustration is not moral, it is logistical: there are beds in the building, and nobody had to be in that room. “Don’t even show up to group if you’re going to be falling over breaking your neck.”

Asked whether sober living works, he gives the honest split rather than the pitch. Yes and no. “At the end of the day, it’s going to be that person who decides their fate.”

Then he stops himself, which almost nobody does. “Can I change my answer? I think it’s global warming.” He talks himself the rest of the way there out loud: fentanyl is bad, but the people are choosing it, and the other thing is not a choice anybody gets.

What follows is the best answer in the Denver block, and it is not an argument about carbon. “The Earth is alive. You and I are both alive. This tree is alive. If the Earth was dead, we wouldn’t be here. We’d be like Mars.” From there it turns into a family relationship: we are all living off our mother, and she has said the same thing every mother says. “The world ain’t going nowhere. It’s going to be right here. So take care of it.”

And then the part that stays with you. “We got to listen to her, and sometimes she might scold us like a parent would do. And who knows what the earth might scold us with? It might be a tornado. She might be like, I told y’all I don’t want that built there.”

A second man is pulled into the conversation and asked what he would put to the entire world. He works his way past the joke to something plain: “We’re all living on a piece of space rock floating in the middle of nowhere. This don’t make any sense, if you ask me. So I’d say everybody just enjoy themselves, live their best life, treat people good, respect each other. Don’t hurt one another, if you can help it.”

It ends with somebody’s ring rolling away across the pavement, which is how these usually end.

Language is unfiltered; the transcript is bleeped where the captions were.
