The creative lead on branding, jargon and AI.

Lee the GOAT, Jos, 12 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: Lee the GOAT, “The creative lead on branding, jargon and AI,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/the-creative-lead-on-branding-a-product-festival-jos. Each transcript line links to its second.

Twelve minutes at ProdFest in Jos, Plateau State, with the person who made it look the way it looks. Lee the GOAT is the creative lead for the event, and he introduces himself at the end of the tape as a creative designer and motion designer. The interviewer arrives having already pressed the organizer on whether the creative side got enough room, and says so, which sets up the rest.

Lee starts with what he actually did: “I actually handled part a majority of the branding”. The brief he set himself was translation. “the creative part for me was to simplify a majority of the web tree terms and jags and processes that you guys always use to make things very complex”. The second half of the brief was geography. The branding also had to communicate “plateau states as a place”, because the point was to pull in “developers that are building in their own separate silos” and get them into a room with each other. He puts the whole design philosophy in one line: “Developers need to understand how designers think and designers need to understand how developers think.”

On what a second edition looks like, he is measured. This was the first time hosting it, and he says “we’ve learned a lot um through the branding and how the branding actually applies in real world spaces”. Next time, more of it and simpler: “we plan to make everything more simpler next year and we plan to have way more creatives here”. He wants illustrators and musicians in the building, not just on the poster, and calls that his personal mission.

The middle of the conversation is the case for the thing he is advocating, and he makes it from his own losses. He was a dancer before he came into the space. Then: “I’ve tried to sell a lot of NFTs right some of them work some of them did not”, and what he took from it was the experience of using the products rather than the sales. His argument for creators is ownership and timing. “Start earning from day one when you are pushing out content.” He runs the list of who that covers: “Think of a musician, think of an artist, think of an illustrator, think of a web designer, think of a social media designer”.

Asked about AI, he does not take the bait either way. “AI is kind of like a new technology like web three that is currently in existence”, and he treats it as a way to enhance a creator rather than replace one. He is specific about the limit: “definitely AI cannot like start everything for you from finish from start to finish”, and if you test the current models you find that “it’s technically impossible or almost impossible for you to envision what is in your head”. Where it earns its place is volume. An illustrator who needs a hundred pieces can use it to make variations, and then “you can pick because you are a human”. The reason a person still has to be there is the whole picture: “You have the entire picture of what the end goal is supposed to look like. AI doesn’t just basically mimics what it thinks you want to see.”

It closes with five quickfire questions. Photoshop for design, Photoshop again for editing, After Effects for video, X for social, Discord for web three. Mobile or camera: “Camera is always better every time.” No hesitation on that one.
