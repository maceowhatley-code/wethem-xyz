Gigi, from Mississippi, on Black women, safety, and what matters most.

Reported by Maceo · Denver · 6 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

Answers What is the most important global issue today? · see how other cities answered →

Asked what the most important global issue is, a woman from Mississippi living in Denver answers in two words and then spends five minutes explaining exactly what she means. Filmed for a weekly bounty that put the same question to people in six countries.

Gigi moved to Denver from Mississippi, and her comparison is immediate and specific. “Denver is livable. I’m speaking from a black woman’s perspective. Always. So Denver is livable to me as a black woman. I feel more safe here.” Mississippi is not dismissed, though. “Being in Mississippi, I feel like that gave me a better foundation for the type of person that I am. Denver is the type of place that you bring your persona to, not where you develop it.”

Then the question, and an answer she knows will draw fire. “I’m probably about to get attacked for this, but I like to speak my mind. The most important issue, globally, locally, at any given time throughout history, unfortunately and fortunately, is black women.”

The argument she builds from it: “We are mothers even when we aren’t mothers. How we are is a reflection of everything. So if we’re not good, nobody’s good, whether they’re pretending or not. If you’re not taking care of us, nothing’s being taken care of.”

She corrects herself mid-answer, unprompted: the daughter she has been talking about is her niece, raised from birth. “She came out into my hands.” That, she says, is the pattern rather than the exception. “So many other black women out there just pouring into people like they’re their kids, and they don’t have no ties to these people. We come from families where even when we are kids, we are mothers, and we just keep doing it until we fall.”

And the line the piece turns on: “Black women are the elders. I don’t care if they’re twelve. If she black, she your elder.”

This was filmed for a weekly hundred-dollar bounty on poidh, where one question went out to contributors in India, Kenya, Nigeria, Las Vegas, New Jersey, Nairobi and Denver in the same week. That format is what One Question grew into.
