A Venezuelan Air Force vet on F-16s and what still makes him smile.

Reported by Maceo, Denver, 9 min

To quote this piece: “A Venezuelan Air Force vet on F-16s and what still makes him smile,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/a-venezuelan-air-force-vet-on-f-16s-and-loving-your-work. Each transcript line links to its second.

A retired Venezuelan Air Force avionics man, new to Denver, is asked what made him smile this week and answers that it was being helped; then, walking to a restaurant he cannot find, he tells the story of the F-16s, the Mirage manuals in French, and the public-school job he never imagined. In Spanish, with some English.

Elios is from Venezuela and has been in the United States since 2023. The question of the week catches him at a good moment: “Soy nuevo por esta zona y es agradable pues es agradable que se sonrió uno porque de alegría, de ayuda.” His question for the world is a practical one: “which is the best country to live?” What he likes here is simple. “All the people, or the majority of the people Respect the law.”

Then the interview walks. Google Maps is no good, the Ethiopian restaurant is somewhere up the street, and on the way he explains what he does now: he works for Denver Public Schools, at West High School since January, and before that at the Denver Center for International Studies, with special-needs students. “Amo mi trabajo,” he says, and then, “Nunca imaginé que iba a trabajar Con alumnos especiales.”

The reason he never imagined it is the previous career. “Yo soy militar retirado De la Fuerza Aérea de Venezuela Trabajé con el F-16 En aviónica de ataque.” He worked on the Mirage too, reading the French manuals (“el francés es relativamente fácil, más fácil que el inglés para mí”), and on the radar systems of the C-130. He retired in 2010, and at his age there was no work at home.

His message for the world is about work itself: “hagan su trabajo con amor, hagan lo que hagan.” And for children, what he learned from his own daughter and from the classroom: listen first. “primero escúchalos abiertamente.”

Filmed by Maceo in Denver. The transcript below is in his own words, mostly Spanish.
