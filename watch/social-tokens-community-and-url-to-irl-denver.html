Social tokens, and a conference for Cape Verde.

Carlos Gomes, Reported by Maceo, Denver, 18 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: Carlos Gomes, “Social tokens, and a conference for Cape Verde,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/social-tokens-community-and-url-to-irl-denver. Each transcript line links to its second.

Eighteen minutes at ETH Denver 2024 with the founder of Forefront, filmed as part of the We Them Media run documenting people of color in Web3. The subject is Carlos Gomes, and this is the first crypto conference he has ever attended.

He introduces himself plainly: “my name is Carlos. I’m from Cape Verde Islands, currently based in New York.” Forefront he describes as an on-chain product studio, doing experiments in media and social graph.

The best part of the week, he says, has nothing to do with panels. He has been in the space long enough that “most of my friends are actually like online friends”, people he works with every day and had never stood next to. Meeting them is why he came.

From there the conversation is community, and he argues it as internet history rather than crypto marketing. “communities are keys to internet for a very long time”, he says, pointing at Reddit and Facebook. What Web3 adds, in his telling, is that the group can hold a financial upside together as well as a group chat. Infrastructure has to work first, but “I think culture and community are arguably one of the most important, it’s like what brings soul to everything we’re doing”.

The middle of the tape is a first-hand history of social tokens, which he was early to. “I’ve always been a creator. I build stuff that I want to see in the world”, he says, and the problem he had was getting people to try the products and report back, so “I wanted to find a way that I could incentivize people to give me feedback”. Then: “I came across a platform called Roll.” They let you mint a creator token. “I was immediately hooked.” He and others doing the same thing formed a group to work out a standard, Seed Club, which he calls an early incubator, the Web3 Y Combinator for what is now consumer crypto.

He is candid about what has not worked. “We always struggle with distribution”, he says of Forefront, which is why he is building on Farcaster now.

Asked what is compelling about the space beyond consumerism, he goes to ownership. “we all know the issues with social media in terms of ownership of the value that you create”, and the case he keeps returning to is the creator who built an audience on a platform that can delete it: “Instagram, they have all the power to take that away”. What crypto should give people, he says, is tools to “own their graph, incentivize their community, distribute ownership of the value the community creates for them”.

The last stretch is home. “I come from a small nation, right? And we don’t have a crypto presence yet”, which is why building often “feels very like I’m on my own”. His answer is a conference: “It’s called ETH Cape Verde. I want to organize a conference of Ethereum conference in Cape Verde.” He says he is working with the Cape Verde government digital economy department, and that the point is developers building solutions that work there. The reason this is his first conference at all is a visa: he lived in China for years and could not easily reach the countries where the conversations were happening. “it is time for us to decentralize that access to conversation.”

On DAOs, he takes the old insult and keeps it. People said “DAOs are basically running on vibes” as a criticism. He thinks vibes are half of it, and the other half is to “build something people want”.
