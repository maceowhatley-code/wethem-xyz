12 years in prison, now building legal tech.

Washington, DC, 27 min, filmed 25 September 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Akeem Rand came to Howard University for Day 1 of the Black Blockchain Summit 2026, where he pitched Pro Se Innovations, a tablet built by his company, Made Investment Group, that gives incarcerated people electronic access to file their own legal motions. Rand says he did 12 years in prison, where he became a paralegal and, in his words, “a conduit” for other people inside: someone others came to for guidance. He talks with Maceo about how prison politics mirror street politics, how people there unify for protection rather than progress, and how entertainment profits from Black culture while excluding the people who make it. Rand explains why he believes crypto can work against inflation rather than feed it, comparing it to fermenting a drink until it is worth something, and argues Black communities could become “untouchable” if they invested in each other instead of outsourcing every problem. Asked about the word in Maceo’s old podcast name, he answers plainly: “we are educated entrepreneurs making a change in society,” he says.
