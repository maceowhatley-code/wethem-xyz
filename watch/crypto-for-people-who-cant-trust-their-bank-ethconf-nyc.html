ETHConf: crypto for people who can't trust their bank.

New York, 6 min · filmed June 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

At ETHConf in Manhattan, June 2026, Maceo meets Cat McGee of the Solana Foundation, from Ireland and living in Buenos Aires, and Juanpi, who does AI consulting for Latin American companies. Cat on who does not get crypto: “a lot of people who are outside crypto who don’t get crypto are people who’ve never experienced problems with their bank or corruption.” Her own case: “I use crypto on a day-to-day basis. There’s a lot of issues with the current financial system in Argentina, and people don’t really trust the peso,” so “most of my money I keep in crypto and stablecoins.” Juanpi, on Argentina: “People that actually want to find a solution, they will connect more with crypto.” For a teenager starting out, Cat’s advice is direct: “don’t start with a centralized exchange,” because “if you don’t see your private key, then it’s not your crypto. You don’t actually own it.” Her name for the idea: “not your keys, not your crypto.” Asked what Solana is, she explains it as “a place for internet capital markets,” then reaches for a bigger idea: the same way crypto learned “not to trust these companies,” she says, blockchains might be how “we trust AI labs to submit all this research and say, yeah, like this is real.” Her summary of it: “maybe that’s a good way to explain what crypto is, that it’s verifiable information.” A note on the facts: Argentina’s inflation was 289 percent a year in April 2024 and 33.5 percent in June 2026 (INDEC); in the 2008 crisis the US Treasury put $245.5 billion into banks through TARP and recovered $275.9 billion by 2023 (US Treasury).
