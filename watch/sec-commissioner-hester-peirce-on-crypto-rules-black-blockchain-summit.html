SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce on where crypto rules are going.

Hester Peirce · Washington, DC · 6 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week →

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce, at the Black Blockchain Summit at Howard University in Washington, DC, on what the agency is doing about crypto. She is named on tape by the host, “thank you for that, Commissioner Pierce”; the roundtable she mentions “in October” places the talk in the autumn of 2025, ahead of its publication in May 2026.

The agency is going out to people who cannot come to it: “we’re also going out on the road, and we’re engaging with people on the road who can’t travel to DC. And really the target there is small crypto companies that are new.” The central policy question, she says, is the line: “this question of where the line is between a security and a non-security is a crucial question for a lot of the other regulatory issues that we have to decide.” Some things have been carved out, and she wants that understood: “It’s not to say that you should assume that there’s no problem in those areas. It’s just you have to understand that the SEC is not regulating those areas.”

She revisits her safe harbor proposal for token launches, “a way for crypto projects to release their tokens to the public,” and says the agency is looking at putting something like it into regulation, though “it probably will not look exactly like what I proposed because the market has changed.” Then tokenized stocks, the stablecoin act and the market structure bill in Congress. Asked how a task force makes sure communities are represented, her answer is an invitation: “If you have something that you think we should be thinking about, you need to tell us.” And: “my door is always open.”
