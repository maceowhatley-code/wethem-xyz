Naira inflation, crypto activism and Africa’s future: Daniel.

Daniel, Reported by Maceo, Abuja, 41 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: Daniel, “Naira inflation, crypto activism and Africa’s future: Daniel,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/daniel-on-naira-inflation-crypto-activism-and-africas-future-abuja. Each transcript line links to its second.

Forty minutes in Gwagwalada, Abuja, at the end of Maceo’s first trip to West Africa, with the person he calls his most active community member. It is the fullest conversation in the archive about what crypto is for in Nigeria, and it starts by comparing two countries. Daniel has just been in Nairobi: “shout out to the Kenyan government for allowing the use of crypto and supporting blockchain brands wherever they could,” where the brands open offices and “it’s been creating employment,” and where you can talk about it anywhere. “But they were not scared of talking about crypto in the presence of a police officer or whatsoever. Unlike what we have here.” In Nigeria an event cannot even be named: “We can’t talk about crypto or whole crypto conferences in Nigeria. It has to be tagged, blockchain, also neutralized finance, something, but not just crypto, or your event will be shut down.”

Why it matters is the currency. “crypto is something we can actually use to hedge against inflation. We’ve seen the Naira go up several percent double digits in the past four months.” Maceo does the arithmetic from the other side: “I feel like a rich guy here but also like dang it’s kind of scary this is why bitcoin is important,” and then the thing that stops him, people “working for like you call it a shitcoin,” on “less than 30 U.S. dollars being somebody’s monthly wages,” while in America “we’re fighting to even get a $15 hourly wage in some places.”

The middle of it is Maceo saying what the trip did to him. “in America, they almost work hard to make you feel like there’s not any place where it’s ideal to be black,” and against that, what he found here: “I haven’t had my dignity, my humanity, or like my integrity questioned once and I got here and that to me is just like, I’m just not used to not having to worry or think about those things.”

Daniel’s argument to the crypto industry is the sharpest thing on the page, and it is not a complaint about access. It is about extraction. “these guys just want to onboard new users over here in Africa,” and “we don’t get value for it. There’s no corporate social responsibility.” His proposal is concrete: a blockchain brand funds clean water in a village that has none, or literacy, or blockchain education, and tags it, the way a foundation would. “That automatically gives free publicity to them.” And the leverage he thinks Nigeria has: “I want them to see Africa, especially Nigeria as the future And not just the future, but as a big market that no crypto brand should ignore,” because “that next billion users will never happen if the Nigerian market is ignored.”

The last third is what each of them is building. Daniel wants to counter a belief with a cohort on WhatsApp: “most people feel like the only way they can actually make money is through internet fraud,” so “I’m aborting them, telling them that you don’t have to get money,” because “once you have a phone, you can get into Web3” Why crypto over forex: “you can actually start even without a dime in your pocket once you have a phone in your palm.” And he wants to do for Web3 what people do for Nigeria generally, name the ones doing it right: “I want to shine a light on Nigerian brands and also African brands also.”

Maceo closes by naming who got him here, and Daniel closes by naming what the network became: “I feel like we’ve moved past that and we are not just a community, I feel like we’re more like a family.” His trip was paid for by that community. Two of Daniel’s short pieces from the same period are on this site. Language is unfiltered. The transcript below is from the original audio.
