A poet named Phoenix.

What does a poet in Denver say when asked about AI, libel and copyright? He goes by Phoenix, and he answers with Lego bricks, a baby learning to walk and a line he built for it: “AI is artificial. We are the official.”

On words: “Will that building stay there? No. But that’s what words do when they mess with what was already good as it is written.”

On money, because he heard a track made with voices he recognised: “We need to start making paperwork to ensure our copyright that we battled in the seventies. That was for us. That’s the seed. We are the fruit.”

On what a machine cannot do, he points to a baby standing up: “Did it show any fear? Free will in a room full of giants, we are.” Then he reads his own poems from his phone. “I don’t believe in stealing my words. As long as I’m alive, they’re our words.”
