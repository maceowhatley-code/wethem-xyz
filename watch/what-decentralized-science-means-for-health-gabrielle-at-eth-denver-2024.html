What decentralized science means for health: Gabrielle at ETH Denver 2024.

Gabrielle, Reported by Maceo, Denver, 7 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

The last episode from ETH Denver 2024, the series FWB funded, and the most personal one. Maceo, “headed home today,” sits down with Gabrielle, his girlfriend, a scientist working in clinical research, who came along. What she found: “how many people were there, how many different organizations were there, and really just the wide array of people that were there.”

Her highlight was the DeSci Summit. “I had no idea what decentralized science was before coming here. I had gotten a taste through learning about Athena DAO, but I didn’t really understand the bigger picture that they were a part of.” She thanks the group that ran it, DeSci World, and Jelani and Josh, whose interview is on the channel. Maceo admits he was in the same place: “I really didn’t know much about decentralized science either, to be honest.” What convinced him was the comparison speakers drew between science publishing and the music industry: “you do all this research, you put it in the journals, you want it to get there, you finally get there and they charge other people for it. I think is a pretty crazy business model.”

What surprised her, compared with Art Basel, was age. “how many people have been in this space for so long that are our age,” which “makes it feel a lot more obtainable.” The person she did not expect to meet was Thane, a first-year from Howard who won the hackathon at the Black Blockchain Summit and was building “a program that was supposed to make transactions go faster.”

Then Maceo asks how her view of We Them Media has changed. “It’s been great to see you work since we’ve been here,” she says, and credits Julian on the cameras, “so attentive all the time.” What she noticed was the room: “seeing how comfortable you are networking,” and “bringing all of us into a conversation with new people that we’re meeting.” He closes by naming what she is: “you are a scientist working in clinical research and I’m excited to see you get excited about the space that I’m super excited about.” Then the sign-off the channel still uses.
