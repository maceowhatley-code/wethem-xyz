Four engineering seniors ask about NFTs and money.

Washington, DC, 11 min, filmed 26 September 2026

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

On Day 2 of the Black Blockchain Summit 2026, Maceo meets four seniors on Howard University’s campus: Seth Archibald and Joseph, who study electrical engineering, and Daniel and Jesse Montgomery, who study chemical engineering. None of them claims to know much about crypto, and Jesse explains chemical engineering for someone in high school: chemists measure at the bench, “and we scale them up to like 1,000, like 10,000”. Then three of them ask the questions most people have. Daniel: “What exactly makes an NFT valuable? What makes it different from a regular picture?” Seth: “what prevents you from just screenshotting the image?” Joseph asks how you tell a good investment from one that is “gonna get rug pulled”. Maceo answers with inflation, the dollar, a blockchain as “a cloud server that nobody runs,” and NFTs as “people gambling on art.” Daniel sums up money better than the answer did: “I see money as a type of credit that everybody agrees on. So if nobody’s agreeing on it, it has no value anymore.” For the One Question, Jesse asks what motivates people, and Seth asks what defines success: “It doesn’t have to always be about money.” A note on the facts: US money supply grew 51 percent and prices 30 percent from January 2020 to August 2026 (Federal Reserve, BLS); the US set up a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve in March 2025 (White House); Chainalysis’s 2026 index puts Brazil, the US and Nigeria on top and Argentina outside the top 20; Argentina’s prices rose 211 percent in 2023 (INDEC) and Nigeria’s inflation peaked at 34.8 percent in December 2024 (Central Bank of Nigeria); and the dollar, down 6.9 percent since January 2025, is still 57 percent of world reserves (Federal Reserve, IMF).
