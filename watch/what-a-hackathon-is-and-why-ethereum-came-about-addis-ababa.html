A Kenyan developer explains the chain from the ground up.

James Wasonga, Reported by Maceo, Addis Ababa, 10 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

Ten minutes in Addis Ababa during a hackathon week, filmed a few days after the teams formed. The subject is James Wasonga, a developer from Kenya, and most of the tape is him explaining the technology from the ground up to an interviewer who says plainly that he is not technical.

He is ten days in and enjoying it. “It’s very amazing being in Ethiopia”, he says, and what he likes is the room rather than the prize: “meeting new faces, meeting new developers, interacting with the team, getting to build as a team, fail, restart, bring up a new one.” Asked to define the format for people who have never seen one, he says “a hackathon is like a coalition where developers come together” to build “a unique solution from any other solution that aims at solving real-world problems”, the point being to “check on the capability of developers”.

His own route in was through school. “it was on 2022 when I first joined university”, where two people running a developer lab in Kenya “got to introduce me about what the blockchain is all about”. The first thing he learned was Solidity, and the first thing he built with a team was real estate on chain, meant “to enable people throughout the across the world to be able to buy property and lease their property within the chain.”

The long middle is a lesson. “Bitcoin is the first blockchain, the first crypto platform too”, he says, and “the use case of Bitcoin was to enable currency, which was more like a cryptocurrency use case”, while “Ethereum came about to ease the developers to be able to write on chain, to be able to deploy contracts.” He does the web the same way: “in web one, one could only able to read”, then “in web two, you could be able to read, write, and do anything”, and then a third thing where “everything was on-chain, on blocks”, data that is public “but with confidentiality”. His summary is short. “It’s about decentralization and security of data and security of users.”

Asked whether the technology is living up to itself, he answers as a builder. “if I ask a person which chain are you building in”, he says, “they always say I’m building Ethereum”, and “Ethereum is the mother”. Other chains do not bother him, since “once a blockchain developer, you’re capable of shifting to any chain”, and anyway “all chains are good.” On home he is direct: “Blockchain is now advanced in Kenya more than it was before.” Then the camera battery dies and the interviewer says the last thirty seconds were never recorded.
