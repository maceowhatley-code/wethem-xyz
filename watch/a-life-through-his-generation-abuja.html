A life told from poverty to laying block.

Abuja, 9 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Supporting Patrons: Aja Davis and Donna Dalton

To quote this piece: “A life told from poverty to laying block,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/a-life-through-his-generation-abuja. Each transcript line links to its second.

One line up front: the middle of this tape is a marriage he says he was pushed into, and he raises it himself.

Nine minutes in Abuja with a man the interviewer has just met, filmed for the channel and for poidh. It opens with the introduction, “Sorry, sir, what’s your name?”, the answer, “Mr. Loris”, and the question that sets the tape: how life has been “through his time, his generation, how it has changed him.”

It comes in order, with almost no prompting. “I passed through many things”, he says, and names it: poverty, and training that led nowhere. “I’m quite educated, but nowhere to find job.” At the worst of it he slept where he could, “I decided to go to a canteen and drop my load there and sleep in somebody’s canteen on the parking zone till I got my own house on hire in Abuja here.”

Then he went after the trade he had learned. “Let me go and seek from the work I learnt, printing. I’m a printer.” Walking the town produced nothing, “All is promise, promise, promise”, and the interviews went the way they go when the man across the desk feels shown up: “even some of them will interview me, then they find out that I’m perfect more than them, they will just say no.”

The middle of the tape is marriage, and he tells it as a matter of timing. At thirty six he decided to “look for wife”, and a woman he did farm work for “decide to bring somebody for me to marry, which I rejected”. He says he was pushed: “She forced me to marry that woman, and the woman is not my own type.” Three children came. The marriage did not last, “I’m no longer with my wife now”, and he leaves it at “But I’m managing.”

Why meeting anyone was hard is its own story. Thirteen years in deeper life, an usher in the church, “Every time I’m always with the Bible”, the beautiful girls in church all married outside, members told not to go with “carnal people, people of the world”. That leaves the question he asks twice: “Then who am I to marry?” So, “I decided to run away from the church.”

The last stretch is building. He carried block as a laborer and watched how it was done, because “building is most important thing to have in richness” and “If you want to be rich, seek for building, seek for land.” He asked an engineer straight out: “please, you help me, give me this chance and let me be a mason here.” The tape ends with him still planning that next step.
