The first lesson of a four week crypto course.

Reported by Maceo, Online, 59 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Executive producer: C.Y. Lee

To quote this piece: “The first lesson of a four week crypto course,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/crypto-clarity-whats-happening-with-the-money-online. Each transcript line links to its second.

Fifty-eight minutes of a class on a video call, livestreamed as it ran: the free preview of a four week course called Crypto Clarity, taught by Maceo of We Them Media to the group who signed up. He goes round the call first, asking what each person’s opening question would be. Most answer with a version of not much.

The answers are the best of the opening. Rosa Spence wants a use for it: “I would love to be in access to provide this for people who have been impacted by violence and disaster”. His mother is on the line and driving, and says “it seemed like I should take advantage of this thing that you’re doing”. Paulina has kept her distance because of the volatility, and he plays it back: it “seems right now like a giant scary casino to many”. He is pleased with the room: “I’m excited that we got so many blank slates.”

He explains himself before he explains Bitcoin. He came in at the end of 2021, ran a podcast interviewing Black people working in crypto, then found a patron who paid for a year and a half of it, and now has “over 70 different interviews with Black people working in the blockchain industry”. The course is partly how he funds finishing them. The slide is blunt: “for most people, crypto is inaccessible, confusing, and predatory.”

The lesson starts with the dollar, not the coin. “de-dollarization is this premise that the value of the dollar is going down”, he says, then BRICS, reserve currencies since World War II, and that “there’s 21 million Bitcoin. Total that will ever exist.” Asked what that means, he goes to inflation, and the hardest line of the night is about wages: minimum wage sits still for a decade while the money loses value, so “it actually turns out to be a massive tax on the everyday working-class person.”

Marianne remembers meeting blockchain years ago, excited by the anonymity in it: “I was surprised to see that it just became currency.” Paulina asks where Bitcoin stands with the US government, which gets the Genius Act, Trump, ETFs, state reserves, and this: “right now the US, I think, is being very quiet”, because if it announced it was holding Bitcoin then every government holding dollars “would raise their eyebrow and wonder, like, am I going to be late to the party?”

Then the course. Four Wednesdays in January, pods of four, a tax professional he has not booked, wallets set up live, an airdrop, presentations at the end. “A DAO is a community-governed cooperative, you might say.” His own start he credits to a college roommate, back when he “was like working as a busser” and the roommate had made “over $50,000 from NFTs”.

Gabrielle plays three of the network’s films and the audio does not survive the screen share. Someone says it sounds like Alvin the Chipmunk. The last one gets through, shot in Ethiopia with Gloria, from Kenya, who says a pop-up city is “a meetup of Web3 builders, mainly in Africa, to use Web3 as a tool to develop the continent and make it better”. Asked what stood out, Maria gives the sharpest answer on the tape: “Like freedom from the current structures that are controlled by the dominant paradigms of, like, the billionaire class basically, and the ineffective political structures that exist that are like foundationally undermining humanity.”

The last stretch is admin, and it does not go well. The course is $250, the code is COMMUNITY50, the site still shows last year’s dates, and one of them has already been charged in full: “there was no place to put in the coupon code, just so you know.” He promises the $50 back and says the link will work by quarter past eight. Then his first students thank him one at a time and drop off.
