A harness for DAOs, and governance you earn.

James, Reported by Maceo, Online, 48 min

In this video? Ask for a change or have it taken down.

Keep up with new releases. Get the newsletter, once a week

Forty-eight minutes of the podcast, three people on a livestream, mostly about what a DAO is for and whether anyone has actually built one. The guest is James, who is building tooling around a project the tape calls MWA, Mental Wealth Academy. The third seat belongs to Zao, who runs a community of the same name and is introduced as a DAO nerd.

James skips the usual introduction. “So my name is James. I’ve been in this space for about 6 years”, he says, and goes straight to the metaphor he runs for the next ten minutes: “I’ve been institutionalized by crypto”. Asked what he means, he explains that “my prison cells are built out of like thick Bitcoin and Ethereum and blockchain wallets and ERC-20s”, that “I didn’t know that creating this blockchain account was a contract, a smart contract, and that contract is a length of time that has barred me in here”, and that everyone on the call is in it with him: “we’re all cellmates. Imprisoned together.”

Zao takes the other side, politely. He knows people who have “quote unquote gotten out of it”, and on James’s framing the on-chain music work he does would mean “by that statement, I would be bringing people into jail, right?” Nobody resolves it.

Harness is the word James uses, and he uses it precisely. “It’s like a harness. It’s like a harness for DAOs”, he says. Most DAOs are “a contract. And it’s going to be like a website, and there’s going to be voting”, while “a harness actually includes the ecosystem. So like the incentives, the mechanisms, the things that you think are fun, and the boundaries into which you are moving into inside of that biome”. Aragon he puts on the far side of that line: “It has the infrastructure and communicating, but it doesn’t have the incentive alignments.”

Where the idea came from is not abstract. “I have a lot of student debt. You know, college is super expensive”, he says, and describes offices where “there’s people right now, like, backstabbing themselves at work because they’re afraid that they’re all going to get cut because of AI, because of layoffs”. Then he pulls the two contracts together: “That contract, that ERC contract, that length of time is the same as your work contract and your employer.” What he wants instead is “an ecosystem rather than a top-down infrastructure tool”, and he is blunt about why DAOs have not been one: “the way that we’ve seen them implemented historically has been reminiscent of top-down businesses”.

Zao answers with mechanics. His governance runs on “illiquid soulbound tokens”, so that “instead of tying our governance to financial capital, we tie it to social capital instead”, earned at weekly meetings in groups of six, scored on a Fibonacci curve. Asked whether that makes it a DAO, he will not claim it: “No, that’s why it’s not a DAO. It’s just me as a leader bringing it forward, a community.” He repeats what an earlier guest told him, “don’t build a DAO, build a community”, and admits most of the system lives in his head: “a lot of it is tribal knowledge, to be honest with you”.

On AI, James says his team wanted agents voting, ran the game theory, and stopped. “Can the AI scam people, right?” was one of the questions. The conclusion: “It can be used as a tool for fun, but it’s just not there yet.”

The last stretch is a screen share, a Claude session Zao runs against the repo, and a pitch for money in both directions. What James wants from anyone curious is one line: “Just, just use it. Just use the platform.” Then the host asks the room to fund a five hundred dollar Bitcoin Pizza Day bounty, and Zao pledges twenty, once the pot has already reached four hundred and eighty.
