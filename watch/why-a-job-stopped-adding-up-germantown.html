“The numbers don’t add up to work for nobody.” Mr. P on Haines Street.

Mr. P, Reported by Maceo, Philadelphia, 21 min

To quote this piece: Mr. P, ““The numbers don’t add up to work for nobody.” Mr. P on Haines Street,” We Them Media, https://wethem.xyz/watch/why-a-job-stopped-adding-up-germantown. Each transcript line links to its second.

A Germantown business owner works through why a job stopped making financial sense, what he thinks Trump is doing to the country, and the one thing he would want his neighbourhood to own for itself. Filmed on Haines Street, Philadelphia.

Mr. P runs a moving and hauling business and has just opened a hot dog cart, Pit Stop 215. He walks through the arithmetic that pushed him out of employment: “I was working for the trash truck company in Jersey and they was paying me $18 an hour. And when I got my check, it was $671 the whole week. But they don’t understand it cost me $400 to get to work. So at the end of the day, I was doing all that work for $200.” An hour’s drive each way, thirty dollars a day in gas. “At the end of the day I’m paying y’all to work for y’all. I can’t pay my bills like that.”

Which is where the line this piece is named for comes from: “The numbers don’t add up for me to work for nobody.”

On Trump, and he says up front the answer will not be popular: “My whole thing is Trump breaking everything down just so it could be greater. People just don’t want to go through the process. That’s the same thing with business, period.” He stops short of saying it is working. “I don’t know if I believe Trump doing a good job. I just ain’t got no proof.” What he says he respects is the scale of the ambition: “He said he was going to take over the world and I be saying the same thing. I’m not trying to take it over like he did, but I’m trying to take over some type of industry to take care of my family.”

Asked what Germantown should own collectively, he does not hesitate. “Life insurance.” Financial products, held locally. “If everybody get together, there’s no way everything couldn’t be done.”

The conversation ends with a job offer on the hot dog cart, at fifteen dollars an hour.
