One Question · take part

Ninety seconds on your phone. Three beats. That is an interview.

Anyone in the network can film one. You do not need a title or a camera crew: a phone, a street, and someone willing to answer. This page is the whole method. It fits on one screen because the method fits in one take.

The three beats → How to send it
58/150 The count so far. Nine weeks, from Wed 9 September to Wed 4 November. Yours goes on it the day Kafani approves it. The campaign, week by week →
01
The shape

Three beats, in this order, in one take.

The shape is fixed on purpose. It is what makes a hundred and fifty of these read as one body of work rather than a hundred and fifty clips, and it is what lets somebody find a specific person in it years from now.

  1. 1They say who they are. Their name, their age, and where they are from, to the lens, before anything else. This is the part people skip and it is the most important part. It is their credit, it is how the interview is findable, and saying it on camera is how somebody agrees to be in this.
  2. 2They ask their question. Whatever they would actually want put to the world. Not a question you gave them. If they need a moment to find it, give them the moment; the pause is part of the tape.
  3. 3They answer it themselves. Both halves or it is half an interview, and Kafani will send it back.
02
On the phone

What makes it watchable.

  1. •Get close. Sound is the thing a phone gets wrong. An arm’s length away, on a street, is right; across the road is a silent film.
  2. •Hold still, and do not cut. One take, start to finish. Brace your elbows or lean on something. A steady ninety seconds beats a beautiful thirty with a jump in it.
  3. •Face them into the light. Put the sun or the shop window behind you, not behind them.
  4. •Upright or sideways, both work. Film the way you would film anything else on your phone. What matters is that the person fills the frame.
  5. •Let the silence sit. When they stop, wait. The second answer, the one after the pause, is usually the one worth having.
  6. •Say where you are, on camera, before or after. The street, the city, the day. It puts the interview on the map, literally: every place the network has filmed is on one page.
03
Getting it to us

Post it, tag us, done.

  1. 1Post it to X and tag @WeThemMediaHQ. That is the whole submission process, and it works whenever you filmed it, from wherever you are. Put their name, age and where they are from in the post, and their question in their words. Tagging us is how you say yes to it going on our channel; if you would rather it stayed on your own account, say so in the post and we will leave it there.
  2. 2Or bring it to the Wednesday call. Turn up to The Weekly Connect, noon in New York, 5pm in Lagos, 7pm in Nairobi, say you will ask the question this week, and Kafani will follow up with you. The call is the easiest place to start if you have not done one before.
  3. 3Kafani approves everything. One editorial standard across every country, which is the only way an archive like this stays worth reading. An interview that skips the first beat comes back, the same as one missing the second half. She is the person you will actually be talking to.

Where a specific question needs chasing we post it on poidh and whoever brings the answer back gets paid. That is a mechanism, not the reason to take part.

04
What happens next

Their name on the piece, yours on the byline.

It goes on our YouTube with their name on it and yours as the person who filmed it. It lands in the catalogue and in the place it was filmed. You get a contributor page that lists everything you have filed.
