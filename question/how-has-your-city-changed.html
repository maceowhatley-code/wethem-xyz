How has the place you live changed in your lifetime?

Length 7:24 Length 5:31 Length 3:39 Length 5:53

Asked of an elder in a Nigerian market by a contributor who went looking for the longest memories he could find, and asked in Denver of five people who had been there between four months and thirty-nine years.

The Nigerian answer is a structured account of a system that used to deliver and no longer does: education, work, safety, marriage. The Denver answers are shorter and they all say a version of the same thing, which is that the city got bigger and more expensive and that the people who made it what it was cannot afford it now.

Who answered, and what did they say?

These are the speakers’ own words, as they said them. We have not checked their claims.

  1. Length 7:24

    Graduates who cannot speak two correct sentences; a country moving fast in the wrong direction.

    “some graduates cannot speak two correct sentences … things are changing rapidly and it is not favoring the whole nation at large”

    An elder in the market, Nigeria, Lagos market · The full piece · at 2:16

  2. Length 5:31

    A lot. No more gang banging in Montbello, and rich white folks moving in.

    “Denver changed a lot. … No more gang banging in Montbello.”

    Lil J Money, 39, from Montbello, Denver · The full piece · at 0:55

  3. Length 3:39

    Worse. Less diversity than she grew up with.

    “I think it’s gotten worse. I don’t see a lot of people, if that makes sense, like I used to see, especially with diversity.”

    An overdose prevention worker, Denver · The full piece · at 1:36

  4. Length 5:53

    The streets used to be a lot better.

    “these streets used to be a lot better than what they are now, man.”

    The Clearinghouse Bandit, back on his old corner, Denver · The full piece · at 1:58

  5. Length 12:15

    Just bigger.

    “it’s just bigger, man. Like it’s sprawled out all over the place.”

    Jake, from Littleton, Denver · The full piece · at 1:50

  6. Length 14:38

    More homeless people on the block.

    “it just feels like there’s more homeless people on my block, but that’s just my block.”

    Five years in, near Pearl Street, Denver · The full piece · at 3:56

  7. Length 10:03

    Gentrifying; the kids he grew up with are gone, and he kept his mother’s house.

    “The negative impact is that all of the kids I grew up with is now gone. I had the liberty of owning my house. I paid taxes like my mama taught me and I still got my mama house.”

    Ranier, 33, North Philadelphia · The full piece · at 0:41

  8. Length 2:26

    An $850 two-bedroom, then the influx and the money.

    “we rented a two bedroom, one bathroom apartment. It cost us about $850.”

    James, from Virginia Beach, a decade in Denver · The full piece · at 1:47
