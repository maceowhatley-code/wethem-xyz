A We Them Media street interview programme

One question, asked everywhere.

One question, put to people on their own streets and answered on camera, in as many places as the network reaches. The point is that somebody in Lagos and somebody in Philadelphia end up holding the same question, and can hear how the other one answered it.

Updated October 12, 2026  |  Week 06 of 9  |  58 people asked on camera

We ask
If you could ask people around the world one question, what would you ask them?
Then, before they walk away

“Now answer it yourself.”

Every question we collect leaves with the first answer already attached to it, given by the person who asked it.

01
What it is

The same question, in as many places as we can reach.

Most reporting picks a place, goes there, and asks whatever the story needs. One Question fixes the question first, then puts it to people on their own streets in as many places as the network reaches, in the same week. What comes back is the same question answered in Lagos and Nairobi and Philadelphia at once.

The question we ask is a question about questions, which is the part that makes it work. Nobody has to know anything about us, or about crypto, or about journalism, to answer it. They only have to have wondered something about somebody else.

Then we ask them to answer their own question, on camera, before they go. That is not a bonus clip, it is the point. You cannot ask the world something you are not willing to answer yourself, and asking for it in the same breath means nobody gets to pose a question they have not thought about. It also means every question enters the archive with an answer already on it rather than hanging there empty.

So each interview produces two things: a question worth putting to strangers, and the first answer to it. What we end up holding is a map of what people want to know about each other, with each person’s own attempt attached.

It runs continuously rather than campaign by campaign. It grew out of #QueryTheWorld, which is now the hashtag we file under rather than a separate programme. Every interview is approved by Kafani before it goes up.

02
How it grows

Every call recruits the next week’s askers.

The Weekly Connect is where the next round of interviews gets commissioned. Out loud, from the people who showed up. That is the engine, and it is the reason the call happens every week rather than when there is news.

  1. 1The week’s interviews go live and land in the catalogue below.
  2. 2Wednesday, on the call, that batch gets played. People watching react to it in real time and the feedback goes back to whoever filmed it.
  3. 3Attendees are asked to go out and ask the question themselves. That week, on their own street, with whatever camera they have.
  4. 4Kafani writes down who said yes and follows up. The follow-up is her named job and a weekly task on the tracker, so a yes given on a call gets asked about during the week.
  5. 5What they bring back plays on the next call, in front of the people who will be asked next. Then it goes again.
  6. 6The questions themselves become the next thing we carry. A question collected on a street in Lagos can be put to somebody in Philadelphia with the Lagos answer beside it. That is the whole idea.

If it works, the number of people asking grows every week without anyone being hired, because the call is doing the recruiting. If it does not, the catalogue below will show it plainly, which is why the catalogue is on the same page.

04
The show

Every Wednesday, on the record.

Live · Wednesday 30 September · from noon New York

The money system we live in.

My grandma told me my whole life to save money. Then a friend explained inflation to me, and I realized “my grandma’s just been burning money by saving money.”

In April 2025 the US dollar was on one side of 89% of all the currency trades in the world, by the central banks’ own survey. So this conversation is yours, wherever you watch from.

We play what people in Philly, Lagos, Abuja and Washington told us, and we talk it through live. No economics degree needed.

12:00 New York · 17:00 Lagos · 19:00 Nairobi · 17:00 London · 16:00 UTC

We start at noon New York and may keep going through the day. Before we start and after we wrap, the player shows whatever YouTube has for our channel. If it says the video is unavailable, we are not on.

Watch on YouTube · RSVP on Discord · The videos behind it

Want in? Film your own. Ask someone on your street what they know about inflation, then ask them the One Question. Anyone with a phone can do this. Send it in by Wednesday 28 October. One entry wins the bounty, we play entries on the stream where they fit, and every one that counts goes in the archive with your name on it. How to film it and enter. Godspeed, friend.

The One Question Weekly Connect is the programme happening out loud. Noon in New York, 5pm in Lagos, 7pm in Nairobi, 16:00 UTC, and we hold ninety minutes for it. It is the community call we ran last year, on the same Discord event, with a programme attached to it. You do not need to have filmed anything to come. Most people who end up filming started out watching.

Every one plays tape. Some of it is what the community brought back that week, and some of it comes out of five years of archive, because a street interview shot in 2023 is still a street interview and the conversation it starts is still worth having. We react to it together, give each other feedback, and cover the rest of what We Them Media has running.

These are live streams rather than a produced show. It is a community townhall as much as a programme call: supporters, contributors, people in the Discord, people who only read the newsletter. Same room. Turn up, watch, say something.

If you came to a community call last year, this is that room. Nothing about it is starting from scratch. What is new is that there is now a programme running through it and a reason to bring something back each week.

The conversation happens first and the community round comes after it, on purpose. Turning up to a call and being asked to introduce yourself before you know what the thing is puts the work on the wrong person. Watch first. Get oriented. Say something when you have something to say.

Every one of these gets recorded and published. We are back to building in public, which means the thinking happens on the call rather than in a document nobody sees.

06
Taking part

What an interview is made of.

Every One Question interview has the same three beats, in the same order, filmed in one take on whatever camera you have. The shape is fixed on purpose. It is what makes a hundred and fifty of these read as one body of work rather than a hundred and fifty clips, and it is what lets somebody find a specific person in it years from now.

  1. 1They say who they are. Name, age and where they are from, to the lens, before anything else.
  2. 2They ask their question. In their own words, with no question supplied by the filmer.
  3. 3They answer it themselves. Both halves, or Kafani sends it back.

In one of the videos? Message us on Instagram (@we_them_media) or X (@WeThemMediaHQ) and we will get back to you within two weeks. The panel headed Something wrong here? at the foot of the page it is on works too. If you want it down, it comes down: you do not have to give a reason and nobody will ask you for one. That holds for anybody in a We Them Media video, whenever it was filmed and whoever filmed it.

Getting it to us

The method in full, the three beats and how to send one in, is on one screen at wethem.xyz/film.

  • Anyone in the network can film one. A phone, a street, and someone willing to answer.
  • Kafani approves everything. One editorial standard across every country.
  • Post it to X and tag @WeThemMediaHQ. It goes on our YouTube with their name on the piece and yours on the byline; if you would rather it stayed on your own account, say so in the post.
  • Or bring it to the Wednesday call. Say on The Weekly Connect that you will ask the question this week, and Kafani will follow up with you.
  • Some are commissioned. Where a specific question needs chasing we post it on poidh and whoever brings the answer back gets paid. This season it is inflation and the One Question, open until 28 October.

Program coordinator

The person you’ll actually be talking to.

Kafani is the One Question Community Steward. She takes the names on the Wednesday call, follows up with the people who said yes, and approves every interview before it goes up. If you want to ask something, or you have filmed something and do not know what to do with it, she is who you are looking for. Here she is, so you know who that is before you say anything.

Directions · for Kafani

Ninety seconds, filmed on your phone.

Kafani, this is the last thing this page needs. Not an interview and not a shoot. You, your phone, one take, about a minute. It sits where people decide whether to take part, so it is doing one job: making you a person they would message rather than a name on a site.

Four things, in this order. Say them however they come out. If you fluff a line, keep going rather than starting again. The unpolished version is the one people believe.

01

“I’m Kafani, the One Question Program Coordinator, and I’m filming this in…”

Name, title, and where you actually are right then. Say the title, because it is what tells somebody you are the person to bring things to. Then the place, which matters just as much: the whole premise of this is somebody standing somewhere specific and talking to whoever is there.

02

“My vision for One Question is…”

What you think it is for and where you want it to go. If what you say and what this page says are different, the page changes, not your answer.

03

“Here is what I have learned doing street interviews…”

One thing you would tell somebody about to walk up to a stranger for the first time. Somebody deciding this week whether they can do it wants to hear that from a person who already has, not from a website.

04

“Come to our weekly call. Wednesdays, five o’clock Nigerian time.”

Say the time the way you would say it to somebody standing next to you in Lagos. Five o’clock your time, not noon somebody else’s. Most of the people who will watch this are closer to you than to New York, and a person saying a time in their own timezone is an invitation while a table of timezones is a schedule. Then say the part that matters most: they do not need to have filmed anything to come, and they can just watch. Then the one line that turns a viewer into a contributor: film one where you are, post it to X, tag us. Say it last so it is the thing people are still holding when the video ends.

Two practical notes. Shoot it landscape, and find a wall or a street rather than a ceiling. Do not script it: the looser it is the better it works.

Once it is up, this block of instructions comes off the page and your video takes its place.

Come to the next Weekly Connect

Wednesdays. The call is the whole thing: that week’s interviews get played, people react to them out loud, and the next round gets asked for in the room. You do not need to have filmed anything to turn up. Come on whichever of these you already use.
